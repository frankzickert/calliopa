## Purpose

- This document is the authoritative description of Calliopa's block editor view: how a document is presented for reading, how a single block becomes editable in place, how edits and structural gestures reach the graph, and which action surfaces the first delivery carries.
- `CA_0008_FEAT_block-editor-view` is the originating change.
- [Workspace View Types](../workspace/view-types.md) owns the view contract, the registry, and the host boundary. This document owns only what the block editor view is and does inside that boundary.
- The stored document and block vocabulary belongs to [Block Document Model](./block-document-model.md). Nothing here defines stored meaning.
- The block editor is the first real registered view type. The placeholder views stay registered for the story-development target kinds that have no real view yet.

## Presentation

* A document opens as a reading surface with no block active. Reading presentation is the resting state, not a mode the user selects.
* Activating a block turns that one block into an editor in place. The rest of the document stays in reading presentation; it is never replaced by a form.
* At most one block is active per tab.
* Reload reconstructs the document entirely from the graph. No editor-specific serialized document tree is stored anywhere.
* The block's own edges carry only the gestures that move the block within the document. Every other control acting on the active block belongs to the bar at the top edge of the editor area.

- Rich text renders in its supported roles, and dividers render as authored content rather than as a gap.
- A stored block type the view does not draw shows a visible unsupported-type fallback. A block is never silently omitted.
- Activation is reachable by pointer and by keyboard, and the activation affordance carries an accessible name.
* The bar stands whenever the view holds a document it could read, in reading and in command mode, drawn by the shell from the groups this view contributes ([Layout](../../../../../../docs/system/workspace/layout.md#the-view-bar)). The groups are, in order: *Work* — *Work in a proposal* (`git-branch`) and `calliopa-refine`'s *Establish…* (`lighthouse`); *View* — icon toggles, retired blocks (Phosphor `archive`), discarded blocks (`eye-slash`), prompts (`terminal-window`) and proposed changes (`git-pull-request`), each `aria-pressed`, named by what it reveals and pressed by more than colour; then, only while a block is the subject, *Format*, *Turn into*, *Block*, *Standing* and *History*, each its own group; and, trailing, *Document* — *Take back* (`arrow-counter-clockwise`) and *Delete* (`trash`), destructive, asking first. With no block active the block groups and their separators are absent and nothing else moves. A view that could not read its document contributes no group. User decision, 2026-09-18 (`CA_0053`).
- Reading is still the resting state: at rest the bar carries what the reader sees and what acts on the document, and nothing that acts on a block, since no block is active.
- The bar was walked on the served build at pin 101 on a desktop and a phone, and the user accepted it on 2026-09-18 (`CA_0053_007`): the *View* and *Document* groups at rest, the block groups arriving on activation and leaving with *Done editing* with nothing in the document moving, each toggle revealing what it names, *Delete* asking first, the link's address field, and the trailing group at the phone's edge while the rest scrolls.
- The bar renders over the surface rather than in the editor area's flow, so showing and hiding it moves no document content. The space it lands on is the headline's own, reserved at the top of the scrolling surface whether or not a block is ever activated, rather than a slot held for a control that is not there (`CA_0031_001`).
- The bar spans the editor area's width, is opaque, and does not wrap. Controls that do not fit the width scroll sideways, so the bar is one row high on every viewport, on touch as on a desktop.
- At a width too small for its groups the whole bar scrolls, with nothing held at an edge, and an
  end that has groups beyond it fades outwards
  ([Layout](../../../../../../docs/system/workspace/layout.md#the-view-bar), `CA_0060`). It is what
  makes the document's own controls reachable on a phone: reading, the groups that stand whatever
  the reader is doing need 430px of a 360px viewport, so a *Document* group held at the edge left
  the block's groups 137px to scroll through.
- The bar is docked to the tab region. The editor negates the region's published inset on the three edges the bar meets and puts the reading inset back on the surface, so the bar's top, leading, and trailing edges are the region's own and the document keeps the line length it had; only the vertical arrangement changed. The bottom edge keeps the region's inset, because nothing docks there and the gap holds the document off the command dock. A view that could not read its document renders a notice and no bar, so it docks nothing and keeps the region's inset as every other view does (`CA_0027_002`).
- The document therefore reads from the top of the region, so what the bar covers is content rather than the inset above it, and the scroll margin a block row carries is exactly the bar's height rather than an approximation of it (`CA_0027_002`). The headline is not among what it covers: the surface's top padding is the bar's height plus a block row's clearance, so with the document at the top the bar lands on that space and the title stays readable with a block active. The space is padding inside the scrolling element rather than an offset starting the surface lower, so the surface still reaches the region's top edge and the document still scrolls under the whole bar (`CA_0031_001`).
- The docked bar carries one border, on the edge facing the content, and no radius, the way the command dock does on the same raised surface. Its top edge needs none because the header already draws one and a second line a pixel below it would read as a doubled seam; its sides need none because they are the region's edges (`CA_0027_003`).
- No control in the bar is disabled for want of a block. A control is disabled only where it is genuinely unavailable, such as undo with no history.
- A block row carries a scroll margin of the bar's height, so a scroll that reveals a block stops below it rather than under it, and activating a block already clear scrolls nothing.
- The editor area shows the document's headline, then its blocks, and nothing else of its own. No tag naming the view renders above a document; the tab strip already says which view a tab holds.
- The document's title is that headline, presented at first-level scale so it reads as the document's name rather than as a field above the document. It is edited in place where it is read, and it holds text only: a pasted value is reduced to its text, so marks, links, and newlines never reach a title.
- The title is the only visible heading above the document, so it takes the level directly beneath the page heading and a document's own heading roles sit below it. The outline skips no level on the way down.
- The title carries the clearance from the bar a block row carries. It is tabbable, so focus reaching it from further down the document scrolls it into view, and the margin stops it below the bar rather than under it. One expression governs the bar's height, a block row's clearance, and the headline's, so none of the three can drift into the others. A wheel or touch scroll that stops mid-title is untouched: that is content passing under an opaque bar, which is what the bar is for (`CA_0031_002`).
- An inactive block shows its content and nothing else. No hover border, no control gutter, and no per-block chrome renders around a block nobody is editing, and the box the active state uses is reserved transparently, so activation changes colour and never geometry.
- The reading block is itself the activation affordance: reachable in tab order, carrying an accessible name, and activated by Enter or Space. There is no separate edit control beside it.
- Activating a block by click lands the caret at the character the pointer landed on, resolved before the block is repainted, so a click inside marked or astral text lands where the reader sees it. Keyboard activation lands at the end, having no pointer position to honour.
- A drag selecting text inside one reading block activates it with that range still selected, so selecting a phrase and formatting it is one gesture. A drag crossing block boundaries activates nothing: at most one block is active, and the reader who dragged across two of them was copying.
* On a phone a first press focuses; a second edits — on a pointer that hovers one click edits (`DO_0004_001`, user decision 2026-09-18, below). Within reading, one block may be *focused* — the tab's fact, like the active block, cleared when the tab is left, by entering command mode and by activation, never persisted. A press on a reading row focuses it and reveals its depth affordance; a second press, Enter, or a key that would type activates the editor, so reading a document's depth costs no accidental edits. Keyboard focus on the row focuses it as well, so tabbing through the document reveals the affordance block by block. A drag selecting text still activates at once, as today, and while another block is being edited a press on a reading row hands the editor over at once, so a phone's keyboard stays open (`CA_0045_003` holds). Reading and command stay the surface's only modes; focus is a fact within reading, as the active block is. User decision, 2026-09-13 (`CA_0046_001`, over keeping the first press an activation with focus on a modifier or a hover-only affordance a phone does not have).
- `state.focusedBlockId` is the fact and `focus$` the one place it is set; a pointer press records itself on the row (`depth.pressing`) so the focus event a press takes is left to the click to decide, while the focus event a keyboard reaches the row with focuses at once. A press outside every row and its depth lets the focus go (the surface's `away` listener), a mode change to command clears it (a task on the mode), and `adopt$` clears it as it puts the editor on a block. A printable key on a focused row activates at its end; the key itself is not typed, since the editor mounts after it. The row says `data-focused`, and the ring is an outline in the selection colour, visibly neither the active block's accent border nor command mode's dashed mark (`CA_0046_001`).
* Switching the edited block keeps the on-screen keyboard open and repaints only the blocks it changes. A press on another block, the arrow keys across a boundary, a merge and Enter hand the editor from one block to the next, so focus passes from one editable block to the next and never through anything that is not editable. User decision, 2026-09-11 (`CA_0045_003`).
- Every gesture that ends in another block hands the editor over in one step: the document the gesture read and the block it leaves the reader in are taken together, so the render that removes one editor mounts the next and the page never renders without one. Idling the editor before a read is what closed a phone's keyboard: the render in between had no editor for as long as the read took, and the focus fell to the page (`CA_0045_003`).
- A touch on another block's words while one is being edited takes no focus, so the editor that has it keeps it until the tapped block's editor takes it over. A mouse press still does, so a desktop drag inside a reading block still selects what it crosses. The outgoing editor keeps the focus while its save runs, and a refused save still keeps the reader in the refused block (`CA_0045_003`).
- A gesture reads the document at most once and replaces the document in hand once, and a row whose revision did not change keeps its DOM node. The row being edited stays the same element while it is edited, whatever its own saves wrote (`CA_0045_003`).
- No bar renders above a block. The editor area has one bar, at its top edge, and the only controls placed on a block are the two that move it: the drag handle on its leading edge and move up and move down on its trailing edge, both present only while that block is active.
- The grips are out of the row's flow. The row holds a gutter open on its leading side on every viewport, whether or not the block is active, so a grip appearing changes colour and never geometry and the drag handle always sits beside the block's text rather than over it. On a desktop the arrows sit in the trailing gutter; on a phone the row gives up its trailing gutter and the arrows stand on the block's top border (`CA_0045_002`). One number governs both the room reserved and the box the grips sit in, so the leading side cannot drift (`CA_0029_001`).
* On a phone, a block's text runs across the full width of the screen. The only margins are a small one on the left for the drag handle, and a hairline of 0.25rem on the right, so the active block's accent border stays off the screen edge. User decision, 2026-09-11 (`CA_0045_001`).
- On a phone the gutter is 1.25rem, about 20px, and everything standing in it fits it: the handle, command mode's reference number and a proposal's face. The handle's hit area is the gutter's width and 44px tall, down the row, so a finger finds it without it taking width. The headline keeps its left edge on the text's. Measured in Chromium on the editor's own markup at 360 and 390 CSS px: the text box starts 20px from the screen's left edge and ends 4px from its right, where it started at 52px and ended 24px from it; the headline starts at 20px; the handle is 20 by 44px at the screen's edge and meets no text; the standing glyph and a two-digit reference number are 20px wide and clear of the words; and a desktop's geometry is unchanged (`CA_0045_001`).
* On a phone, move up and move down sit on the active block's top border at its trailing end, and never cover its text. Their upper part may cover the right end of the last line of the block above, which is not being edited; that overlap is accepted. User decision, 2026-09-11 (`CA_0045_002`).
- Measured the same way: the arrows' box, 20px tall, ends on the active text box's top edge and meets none of its text, and reaches 10px into the box of the block above. A block activated from under the bar brings its text and its arrows clear of it (`CA_0045_002`).
- Retire, insert paragraph, and insert divider act on the active block from the bar at the top edge of the editor area. Their group is there only while a block is active, so none of them ever names nothing, and each keeps naming the block it acts on.
- Reordering by pointer drag begins from a block that has been activated, because the handle is one of its grips. Keyboard move stays available through the other, and both still compile into the same atomic move mutation.
- A drag can land below the last block. The area below the last block is a drop target accepting a move, the mark reserved there lights while the pointer is over it, and the drop compiles into the move that places the block at the end. That area carries both gestures: it is the way into writing below the document, and a release ending a drag is not the click that starts writing, so one press gets one answer (`CA_0029_002`).
- A completed move leaves the block active with the caret it had, for the pointer drag and for both move arrows alike. Repositioning a block is part of writing it, not a reason to stop. The caret is read before the move is sent, because the move re-reads the document and hands the block back from that read (`CA_0029_003`, `CA_0045_003`).
- Keyboard reach runs handle, text, arrows, which is the order the reader sees them in.
- Repositioning is the one operation whose meaning is spatial, which is why it is the one that earns a spatial position. The rule decides where a later control goes, so neither position drifts into carrying the other kind of operation.
- [ ] Give a divider a way to move. Activation is what puts the grips on a block, and only a text block can be activated, so a divider has neither the drag handle nor the move arrows and cannot be repositioned once inserted. Deciding this means deciding what a block that can be moved but not typed into is, since activation currently means that a block has an editor. This line carries no task identifier because no change document owns it yet.
- A document that says nothing reads as a blank page: no blocks, or one text block with no text, shows a placeholder across the block area rather than a row reporting that a block is empty. Clicking it starts writing, and clicking below the last block of a document that does have content opens a paragraph directly below the lowest drawn row ([A New Block Lands Below](#a-new-block-lands-below)) — from reading; with a block active either press only ends the edit. A trailing empty paragraph that is the lowest drawn row is activated rather than followed by another, so repeated clicks cannot stack blocks that say nothing.
- A proposal shown is something the document has to show, so an empty document with a proposal on screen is drawn with its blocks and the proposal among them, never as the blank page. A document is created with one empty block, so the first thing a reader asks of a new document meets this case: the toggle, or a run's end that turns it on, showed a blank page and nothing else (`DO_0002`).

The margin and the activation scroll are in place, and `CA_0014_002` has since bounded the regions, so the editor's surface now scrolls under a bar that stays put and the scenario is reachable. `CA_0027_002` makes it worth more: once the bar covers the document rather than the inset above it, a block activated near the top is genuinely underneath.
- A press that lands outside the active block ends the edit, returning the document to reading presentation exactly as `Done editing` leaves it. Outside is anywhere in the shell — the inspector, the header, the panels — not only the document tab, so a reader reaching for another surface commits the block and closes it before that surface takes the caret (`CA_0028_001`).
- The bar is not outside, because every control in it acts on the active block, and neither are the block's own grips: they are its edges. A gesture that began inside the block is not leaving either, which is what lets a selection dragged out past the block's edge and a reorder drag released anywhere both keep the block open (`CA_0028_001`).
- The tab strip and the library are not outside. A press on either navigates to another tab, and [Workspace Shell](../workspace/tabs.md) fixes that a tab retains its own selection; ending the edit there would clear the very block a returning tab comes back to. The unmount flush commits what was typed on the way out, so nothing is lost by not leaving (`CA_0028_001`).
- The gesture is a click rather than a pointer press. A touch scroll begins with a pointer down on the reading surface and produces no click, so the browser already separates the tap that means leave from the scroll that does not, and ending on release leaves the browser's own focus placement intact. A pointer press records where the gesture began, so a click landing on a common ancestor is judged by both its ends (`CA_0028_001`).
- The listener is the surface's own, document-level, beside the keydown listener it already owns, with the same inert guard and the same cleanup. One place decides a press: a surface that answers its own — another block's reading row, which activates itself and commits the outgoing block on the way, and the blank page and the area below the last block, which are buttons whose handlers already run — is left to answer it alone (`CA_0028_001`).
- Focus moving out of the block by keyboard leaves it active. `Escape` is already the keyboard's way out, and tabbing to the bar's controls is how the keyboard uses them (`CA_0028_001`).
- Leaving happens once. The editor records that it is under way before it awaits anything, so two surfaces answering one gesture cannot each save from the same base and have the second refused as a conflict the reader never caused (`CA_0028_001`).
- The blank page and the area below the last block are ways into writing from reading. With a block active a press on either only ends the edit, like every other press outside the block, and writing there takes a second press (`CA_0028_002`).

## Depth

* Depth is invisible while reading and discoverable while interacting. Focusing a block reveals only the categories that hold material — *What this rests on · What rests on this · Where this came from* — and what one opens unfolds beneath the block, each category adding a distinct kind of meaning, in that fixed order and only where material exists, with *Focused work* beside them. No empty section, no counts, no permanent inspector; no category restates another (`BO_0243`, the material's §11–§14; `CA_0046`, `BO_0258_009`).
* The block's visual anchor is preserved: expanding moves the focused block by nothing, the expansion unfolds below it and the viewport compensates (`CA_0046`).
* A block's claim revisions are read in *Where this came from*, drawn only on request or when a material change to one of the block's claims is on record (`BO_0248`), listing the revisions with who and when. User decision, 2026-09-13 (`CA_0046`, over a command answered in the console), under the three categories of `BO_0258_009`.
* Derived material is visibly derived and challengeable: a frontier, a tension, a viable alternative, a projected consequence or a next move says on focus that it is derived for this root, from what, and can be fixated or challenged from there. `BO_0246` carries what fixating and challenging do beyond the standing and the composer.

- The place and its geometry are this extension's; what fills it is `calliopa-refine`'s (`BO_0256_007`, `BO_0258_009`). The editor renders the `depth` place for a block that is focused and not being edited, and knows nothing of what an extension draws there; the categories, what each holds, the icon row that opens them and every judgement surface below are refine's `docs/system/system.md`. `lib/depth.ts` keeps only the primitives refine composes — `evidenceOf`, `relevanceOf`, `orphanedOf`, `provenanceMatters`, `KIND_WORDS`, `REVERSE_KIND_WORDS`, `DERIVED_SECTIONS` — and no layer model of its own.
- The verification and walk records below are dated, and the surfaces several of them name have since moved: the depth's components and their tests are `calliopa-refine`'s since `BO_0256`, and its categories are the three of `BO_0258_009`. They record what was checked when it was checked, and are not restated to match.

## Editing And Saving

* The graph document model is authoritative. Editor state is transient.
* A save creates a graph revision of the affected block and returns the new baseline revision.
* Navigation or a tab switch must not silently discard unsaved input.

- Editing a block schedules a save after a short typing pause. Leaving the block, or leaving editing mode, flushes any pending save immediately rather than waiting for the pause to elapse.
- There is no explicit save gesture. The user never presses save to keep their work.
- Equal normalized content is not written again, so a pause that changed nothing produces no revision.
- Saves of a tab go one at a time: a save that arrives while one is on its way — the pause's save and the save a leave flushes can fall in one breath — waits for the answer and compares against what it saved, so the block is written once. Sent together, the second carried the same base and the kernel's per-node floor refused it as `write_too_frequent`, leaving the block unsaved (found live in the `BO_0248` walk-through, 2026-09-14). `saving`, a module-level map per tab beside `inFlight`, holds the save on its way, and `handover.test.ts` proves the leave during a pending pause save writes once.
- The tab carries the save state — saving, saved, or unsaved — and the shell header renders it for the active tab, so a reader learns whether their work is safe whether or not a block is active. A bar present only while a block is active could not carry it.
- A refused save names itself on the affected block. The tab's state says the graph does not hold what was typed; only the block can say which block.
- Rich text, links, and paste resolve into normalized runs carrying supported semantic marks. Editor-specific serialized state is never stored.
- [ ] Move the editor's notice clear of the bar. The notice renders in the editor area's flow at its top, which is where the out-of-flow bar sits, so a notice raised while a block is active is covered by the bar that only appears while a block is active. This is true today and `CA_0027` does not cause it, but docking the bar over the document makes it certain rather than incidental. Decide where a notice goes when the surface's top edge belongs to the bar. This line carries no task identifier because no change document owns it yet.
- Leaving a block re-reads the document only when the document in hand is behind. A save writes without reading back, so what is held is stale exactly when this session wrote something, which the revision says: the editor's base has moved past the one the document was read at. A block nobody changed leaves them equal and the round trip and its repaint would buy nothing. A refusal re-reads too, because the graph then holds someone else's text and the reader is about to be shown the block (`CA_0028_004`).
- A refused save outlives the block it happened to. The reason is named on the affected block while it is active, and a press that leaves carries it to the view's notice, which is readable with no block active, so the tab's unsaved state says why and where rather than only that the graph is behind. Leaving is never blocked: a reader who cannot leave a conflicted block has no way out until `CA_0008_012` gives them one (`CA_0028_005`).

## Conflict

* Concurrent or stale edits follow the conflict behavior in [Graph Gateway](../content-store/graph-gateway.md): the mutation names its base revision, and a stale base refuses the whole mutation.
* The editor surfaces the conflict outcome on the affected block. It must not silently overwrite and must not resolve automatically.

## Structural Gestures

* A structural gesture compiles into one atomic graph mutation whenever partial success would leave the document malformed.

- Enter splits the active block at the caret, and Shift+Enter breaks its line. Backspace and Delete at a boundary merge compatible neighbouring blocks. Arrow keys move the caret across block boundaries.
* Enter splits the block on screen at once. The head keeps the text before the caret, the new block below takes the text after it with the caret at its start, and what the reader types next goes into the new block before the graph has answered. User decision, 2026-09-11, over waiting one round trip for the graph to create the block (`CA_0045_005`).
* A split the graph refuses after the screen has shown it folds back: what the new block holds, typed words included, joins the block it came from at the split point; the editor stays there with the caret at the join; and the refusal is named on it as a refused save is. Nothing typed is lost. User decision, 2026-09-11 (`CA_0045_005`).
- A split is sent after it is drawn, as one command: the split naming the tail the screen already shows, carrying the head's words and role as the editor holds them, so the head is written once (`DO_0015_001`). Splits go one at a time, in the order they were pressed, so a second Enter in a block not yet landed splits on screen at once and its command waits for the first answer — and then the kernel's floor, since it writes the tail and the document node the first just wrote (`CA_0045_005`, `DO_0015_002`).
- Nothing is written after a fold-back. The head holds the joined words unsaved against the base that was refused, exactly as a block whose save was refused does, which is what Conflict requires; a fold-back joins every split still on its way, and a save the tail's typing had scheduled does not carry over to the head (`CA_0045_005`).
* Enter splits the block on screen at once on every origin the instance is served from, plain HTTP at a network address included. The editor mints the tail's identity as a version 4 UUID from `crypto.getRandomValues`, which a browser offers in any context (`lib/block-id.ts` `newBlockId`); `crypto.randomUUID` exists only in a secure context, and its absence had ended the press in a `TypeError` before anything was drawn (`DO_0003_001`).
* Shift+Enter puts a line break at the caret in the block being edited, and the block stays one block. The line break is a character of the block's text, `\n` in the run it falls in ([Block Document Model](./block-document-model.md)). The block is drawn with its lines in reading and while being edited (`white-space: pre-wrap` on `.block-text`), and the caret, Backspace and Delete take a line break as one character, as they take any other. A break at the very end is held by a `<br>` that `runsFrom` never reads back, so the caret has a line to stand on. Paste keeps inserting the clipboard's plain text at the caret, newlines included — a `\r\n` as one — so a pasted text of several lines stays in one block and shows its lines. Ctrl/Cmd+Enter stays the surface's with or without Shift (`BO_0267_012`). The editor is a multi-line textbox. User decision, 2026-09-18 (`DO_0003_003`).
* ArrowUp on the first line of the block being edited leaves it, wherever the caret stands on that line, and ArrowDown on its last line does the same; a line is a line as drawn, whether a line break ends it or the block's width wraps it. The caret lands on the nearest line of the block it goes to, the last going up and the first going down, at the same place across the page, or at that line's end when the line is shorter. Within the block, Up and Down move between its lines as the browser does, and with Shift, Alt or Ctrl/Cmd they are always the browser's. ArrowLeft at the block's start and ArrowRight at its end keep leaving it as they do. The block the caret goes to is the nearest block the document draws for editing, in the order it draws them: a prompt only under *Show prompts*, and never a row that takes no caret — a discarded row, which even under *Show discarded* offers only *Reopen*, a retired row, a proposal row or a divider. User decision, 2026-09-18 (`DO_0003_002`).

- Insert places a new paragraph or divider after the current block.
- Retire removes a block from the document without deleting graph history. It is recoverable from the document-level retired-block list in [Block Document Model](./block-document-model.md), which stays available for the life of the document.
- Reordering is available as a pointer drag and as a keyboard and action-menu move. Both produce the same atomic move mutation.
- Pointer, keyboard, and touch expose the same operations with appropriate gestures and accessible names. No operation is pointer-only.

## Undo

* Undo inside a block is transient editor history over text and marks. It never reverses an already saved structural operation.
* Reversing a saved structural operation is a deliberate, separately named action, never a side effect of the undo keystroke.

- A retired block is recovered through restore rather than through undo.

## Out Of Scope

- The extension manager, graph-hosted source tree, materializer, kernel bridge, release pins, extension review policy, and artifact manifest of the old `calliopa-bootstrap` artifact editor. This view is native application code.
- Collaborative cursors, multiplayer conflict resolution, comments, and offline editing.
- Defining the stored document and block vocabulary, which is [Block Document Model](./block-document-model.md).
- Moving blocks between containers, which needs a container block type no change has introduced.

- A row is keyed by block identity and revision together, so a revised block is a new row rather than the same row with new content, unless it is the row being edited (`CA_0045_003`). The consequence is worth stating because it surprised a scenario in [Workspace Shell](../workspace/frame.md): a row left after a save is replaced by a new DOM node, so anything measuring a row has to tolerate finding it and then finding it gone.

## Implementation

- The editor imports no *value* from a `server/` module. A type import is erased, which is why `lib/agent-at-work.ts` may name `DocumentProposals` and `DocumentActivity`; a value import is a runtime edge, and it pulls that module's `node:` imports into the client bundle, where `AsyncLocalStorage` is not exported by the browser shim and the build fails outright rather than warning. A predicate both sides need lives in `lib/` and the server module imports it back, as `isAgentPrincipal` does — one definition, and the rule stays the server's to state. Found when `BO_0258_016` had the editor read `isAgentPrincipal` from `server/work.ts`, which refused the promotion build at dataRevision 938.
- `src/lib/runs.ts` is the run model, shared by the editor in the browser and the vocabulary on the server. It holds the permitted roles and marks, normalization, splitting, and every edit a selection can make: `applyMark`, `applyLink`, `replaceRange`, `marksAt`, `linkAt`, `sameRuns`, and `readRuns`. One implementation is what makes "equal normalized content is not written again" true: a surface that normalized differently from the boundary storing it would write revisions saying nothing. Positions are character offsets, never code units, so astral text splits where a reader sees the boundary (`CA_0008_004`, `CA_0008_005`).
- `src/server/documents/vocabulary.ts` imports those primitives rather than restating them, so the schema validator and the editor's request parser answer "is this a run list" the same way.
- `src/server/documents/api.ts` is the transport the editor reaches documents through: a read, a retired-block read, a create, and one named command per structural gesture. A request that cannot be read is refused before any operation runs. The routes are `GET /api/x/ui.shell/documents/[id]`, `GET /api/x/ui.shell/documents/[id]/retired`, `POST /api/x/ui.shell/documents` and `POST /api/x/ui.shell/documents/[id]/commands`; reads and edits are separate entry points, so an accidental edit cannot arrive on a read path (`CA_0008_002`, `CA_0008_006`).
- `src/server/outcome.ts` (formerly `src/server/graph/outcome.ts`, `BO_0207`) holds the outcome-to-status mapping both the graph API and the document API answer with, so one outcome cannot mean two things depending on which boundary reported it.
- Every document write answers with the revision it established. `WrittenBlock.revisionId` in [Block Document Model](./block-document-model.md) is what lets the editor save twice in a row without reading the document back between saves (`CA_0008_004`).
- `src/components/views/block-editor.tsx` is the view. `src/components/views/editor-dom.ts` is the only crossing between runs and a live `contenteditable`: it paints runs into an element, reads an element back into runs, and converts between a live selection and character offsets.
- A block is painted imperatively and read back as runs; the model is authoritative in both directions. **What the document's render reads is the design constraint**: a keystroke that changed anything read while the block list renders would make Qwik rebuild the element the caret sits in. The editing state is therefore read only inside event handlers and inside small child components — the bar, the block's refusal notice, the drop indicator — that re-render alone (`CA_0008_003`, `CA_0008_005`).
- `ActiveBlockText` owns the editable element through a ref and paints from its own visible task, so a paint cannot run before the element exists. Mounting is one such paint, which is why activation, a reload, and a structural gesture need no special case.
- A block row is keyed by block identity *and* revision, because a row renders one revision. A revised block is a new row; without that, a text change in place left the old text on screen until a reload. The row being edited is keyed `<id>:editing` instead: it renders the editor, not a revision, and a revision its own save wrote would otherwise remount the element the caret is in. Leaving puts the revision back into its key, so its reading text is the graph's (`CA_0045_003`).
- `adopt$` is the one step that takes a document read from the graph as the one in hand and puts the editor on the block the gesture leaves the reader in, with nothing awaited inside it. `activate$`, `readBack$` (a structural command's read, which `structural$`, `insert$`, `appendBlock$` and `setRole$` share) and the instant split all end in it. `freshDocument$` reads the document for a base without replacing the one in hand, so a gesture replaces that once (`CA_0045_003`).
- The touch focus is cancelled by a document-level `mousedown` listener of the surface's own, beside the ones `CA_0028_001` gave it, when the press before it was a touch (`pointerType`) and a block is active; a Qwik handler's `preventDefault` runs after the default has already happened, so the listener is plain (`CA_0045_003`).
- An instant split draws its tail with the head's role and standing, the revision `pending`, and an order key from `orderBetween`, as the server mints it; `splitting.pending` holds each split drawn and not landed, and `drainSplits$` sends them through `landSplit$`, which folds back through `foldBack$` on a refusal. `inFlight`, a module-level map per tab like the scroll map, holds the sending, and `writesSettled` is what a save, `reload$` and `freshDocument$` wait on before they touch the graph: a read taken while a split is under way would not hold the block the reader is typing in, and a save of a pending block has no revision to name until its split answers with the tail's (`CA_0045_004`, `CA_0045_005`).
- Keyboard gestures read the caret from the DOM at keydown rather than trusting the last keyup: a keyup handler is a lazily loaded chunk, and a split from a stale offset would cut the block in the wrong place.
- Format controls prevent the default mousedown, so focus stays in the text and the selection they act on survives the click.
- A click on reading text is resolved to a model offset before anything repaints, because the repaint replaces the very nodes the point named. The click has already left a selection in the reading text, so `selectionIn` reads it — collapsed for a click, a range for a drag — and `offsetFromPoint` in `editor-dom.ts` is the fallback for a browser that left none. `selectionEscapes` is what makes a drag across two blocks activate neither (`CA_0013_004`).
- Offsets read from the reading element describe the revision the document had in hand. `activate$` clamps them to the block it re-reads from the graph, so a fresher, shorter block cannot put the caret past its end (`CA_0013_004`).
- The reading block carries `tabIndex`, `role="button"`, and its own accessible name, which is what replaced the edit control beside it. Removing that control is what made the resting surface content-only, so the affordance had to move onto the thing being activated (`CA_0013_003`).
- `--block-grip-gutter` is the room a block holds open on each side and the box its grips sit in, as one number: two values that had to agree would drift and leave a grip over the text. `.block-row` reserves it as `padding-inline` whether or not the block is active, and the grips are `position: absolute` at the row's inline edges, so a grip appearing moves nothing (`CA_0016_001`). Under `640px` it is 1.25rem, the row gives up its trailing padding, `.block-surface` puts back none of the region's inset on the leading side and 0.25rem on the trailing one, and `.block-grip--end` stands on the text's top border: `top` is the drop mark's height and the text's margin, lifted by `--block-arrows`, the arrows' height, which a row's `scroll-margin-top` adds to the bar's. The phone's rules come last in `block-editor.css`, so they win over every rule they narrow (`CA_0045_001`, `CA_0045_002`).
- A document that says nothing renders a placeholder in place of the block list rather than an empty row, and `emptyDocument` is what decides: no blocks, or one text block with no text, which is the state a document is created in. `startWriting$` activates a trailing empty paragraph when it is the lowest drawn row rather than appending another, and `appendBlock$` places below that row (`DO_0016_002`), or at `at: "end"` when there is no document read yet, so it is also the way into a document with no block to name (`CA_0013_007`).
- The blank page stands only while `shownProposals` is empty, next to the retired-blocks exception: `placeProposals` draws the items among the reading order's rows, so a blank page in place of those rows hid every proposal, including a system run's derived candidates, which are drawn with the toggle off. `views/proposals/empty-document.test.ts` proves it in the render harness: a document holding one empty block with a rewrite of it and an insert standing reads as the blank page with the toggle off, and pressing the toggle draws both items and no blank page. ## Never Waiting On A Read

- Under `DO_0001`, requested by the user on 2026-09-16, the editor never holds the page while a read or a write is under way. Qwik holds every render the page asks for until a `useTask$` settles, so a task that awaits the network freezes the tabs, the library and the blocks for as long as the network takes; only a native control still answers.

## Standing

* A text block carries one standing on the scale discard · keep · fixate, or the prompt standing *Send* gives it, off the scale ([Prompts](#prompts), user decision 2026-09-18). Keep is the absence of a stored value: where a block stands unless someone says otherwise. Fixate subsumes keep: a fixated block is left standing and also stands behind every command issued in its document. User decision, 2026-09-21 (`BO_0272`, replacing the five-position scale `BO_0227` gave it).
* On touch, a horizontal swipe on a reading row sets it: one action each way, leftward to discard and rightward to fixate, and a swipe back from either returns the block to keep. The row follows the finger, the reveal names what release would commit **from the first movement** — the word beside the finger, quiet until the travel reaches the threshold and then in the ground of what it would do — and a release short of the threshold commits nothing. User decision, 2026-09-21; the naming from the first movement after the walk found it came too late to see on a phone.
* No gesture is the only path. The bar's **Standing** control sets the active block's, `Alt`+`Shift`+`ArrowLeft`/`ArrowRight` steps a focused reading row along the scale, in reading and in command mode, and while reading the row the bar acts on carries its own standing toolbar, which sets it where the row is (`BO_0231`, moved out of command mode by `DO_0014`). Beside that control stands an info control saying what each standing means and how it is set, drawn when the control it explains is (`BO_0272`, moved there from a group of its own by `DO_0010_005`, user decision 2026-09-22).
* Every change of standing can be taken back from *Take back* in the bar's trailing group, which names what it takes back. It writes the previous value: a separately named action, never the undo keystroke. User decision, 2026-09-20 (`CA_0058_011`, replacing the dock's undo line).
* A discarded block leaves the drawn flow and stays in the document and its order; the panel's *Show discarded blocks* draws it where it sits, with *Reopen* ([Document Panel](./document-panel.md)). A fixated block is drawn as a card labelled on its top border, as every marked row is ([A Marked Block Is A Card](#a-marked-block-is-a-card)); a kept block carries no mark at all, since a mark means someone acted. User decision, 2026-09-21.

- One threshold per direction, the same distance each way, measured on the room the finger has — the lesser of the row and the screen: 36% of it within 132–250px and never beyond 72% of the screen. The distance sits between the near and far thresholds the five-position scale had (18% floored at 64px, 55% floored at 200px), since with one action each way there is no long pull left to make discard deliberate and no short one left to make fixate cheap. The floors are the load-bearing half: proportion alone collapsed on a phone whose column measured 172px (`BO_0138`). User decision, 2026-09-21.
- The swipe's table is configuration: the thresholds, the flick and the edge guard are a value `lib/swipe.ts` takes, so another table — two thresholds again, other distances — is a value rather than a rewrite. User decision, 2026-09-21.
- A flick commits what is revealed. A press within 24px of a screen edge is the system's back gesture. A mouse never swipes: a horizontal drag under a mouse selects text.
- The chord never acts on the block being edited, where `Option`+`Shift`+arrow is macOS's word selection; the bar and the block's own toolbar are that block's paths, both of which name it while it is being edited.
- Discarding a marked block keeps its references: a discarded block is markable, and a reference is what was marked (`BO_0263`). Its chip says *since discarded*.
- A standing written on the block being edited saves the edit first and writes from the editor's base, which then advances; nothing under the caret is re-read, and the new standing shows until the document is next read. On any other block, an edit under way ends first, as a press outside would, and the document is read again. Discarding the block being edited ends the edit, since it leaves the flow.
- A row's accessible name says its standing (`row-name.ts`), and each change is announced.

- `lib/disposition.ts` is the scale: `SCALE`, the words (`LABEL`, `DONE`, `MARK`, `MEANING`), the glyphs (`GLYPH` for the mark, `CONTROL_GLYPH` for a control that offers a state, which is why keep has one there and no mark at all), `step` — one press is the whole distance, so the swipe, the chord and a stepping control all commit it — and `RETIRED`, what a value written before the scale narrowed reads as. `lib/swipe.ts` is the physics, pure: `SWIPE`, `thresholds`, `swipeTarget`, `swipeReveal` and `swipeOutcome`, each over a `SwipeTable` (`BO_0227_011`, `BO_0272_006`, `BO_0272_007`).

Reading text declares `touch-action: pan-y` (`BO_0227_011`).
- `src/components/views/standing/`: `use-standing.ts` is the one write every path goes through — `setStanding$`, the overlay, the announcement and the take-back, the bar's *Standing* choice and a proposal's buttons included; `standing-toolbar.tsx` draws `StandingButtons` over whatever write its caller hands them, which is how a proposal carries the same three; `standing-mark.tsx` is `CardLabel`, the label a marked row carries on its top border, and the standing that picks it; `discarded-row.tsx` a discarded block drawn in place; `standing-announcement.tsx` the live region (`BO_0227_012`–`BO_0227_014`).
- A proposed change is drawn as a block among the blocks (`ProposalBlock`), in its proposer's colour with one line on its bottom border — the proposer's face, what it does in words, and its answer icons (`BO_0265_010`) — and its text can be edited, which accepts it, and dragged or arrow-moved by its face, which does not ([Proposed Changes In Place](./proposed-changes.md#proposed-changes-in-place), `BO_0233`). `Marked` and `ROLE_TAG` moved to `src/components/views/block-text.tsx`, so a proposal renders in the typography of the block it would become. - The editor's calls to the documents API moved to `src/components/views/documents-client.ts`, and `readingOrder` and `placeProposals` to `src/components/views/reading-order.ts`, unchanged but for the discarded toggle, so the editor composes these modules rather than holding them (`BO_0227`).

- While reading, the row the bar acts on carries three buttons on its top border, like a tab — Discard ✕, Keep ○, Fixate ◆ — named from `LABEL`, the current standing pressed; pressing the pressed one returns the block to keep. A swipeable proposal and a revealed discarded row carry the same three (`StandingButtons`, below). It is drawn on that row and on no other, rather than drawn everywhere and hidden, so a button that should not be there takes no focus and no rule has to say so; out of the flow, so showing it moves no text (`BO_0231_002`, `BO_0231_004`, `DO_0014_002`).

## Mathematics

- Under `calliopa-bootstrap`'s `BO_0290`, promoted to draft by the user on 2026-09-23 and

  as their source and never as a placeholder replaced once a renderer wakes up. User requirement,
  2026-09-23.
* Editing an equation happens in a popover. User requirement, 2026-09-23.
* The engine is MathJax. User requirement, 2026-09-23.
* Inline mathematics is authored both ways: from a control on the bar with its keyboard shortcut,
  which turns the caret or the selection into an equation and opens the popover, and by typing
  `$…$` in the flow, which converts as the closing `$` lands, with undo restoring the literal
  characters. Pasted text carrying `$…$` converts the same way. User decision, 2026-09-23.
* Every TeX package MathJax's full TeX input carries ships, with on-demand loading off so no render

### Why Nothing Jumps

- An equation moves the page in two ways, and this surface closes both. A renderer that runs after
  hydration replaces a placeholder with typeset output of another size; an output format whose
  glyphs come from web fonts reflows again when the fonts land. Typesetting **on the server, into
  SVG** answers both at once: SVG glyphs are paths and need no font file, and the server-rendered
  response already carries the typeset equation, so nothing arrives later to change its size. The
  reader never sees a placeholder and never sees the source.
- The engine reaches the browser only for editing — the popover's live preview, and a block the
  client redraws after a write — from a lazily loaded module, the way the editor's own attachment
  loads. A reader who never edits never downloads MathJax.
- **Inside the editing surface an inline equation stays typeset**, as one atomic non-editable
  element the caret steps over, the way the anchor behind a link is already an element in the flow.
  This is why the popover exists: were the source to take the equation's place while its block is
  edited, the sentence would reflow on entry and on exit. The retired shell did exactly that,
  forced there by Lexical (`BO_0163`), and this surface refuses it.
- Numbers and references are resolved in the document read, so they arrive in the same response as
  the equations and nothing resolves late either.

### What `BO_0163` Paid For

The retired shell built display and inline mathematics once. Three findings are inherited rather
than rediscovered: MathJax's on-demand loader fetches macro packages from a **CDN** unless it is
switched off; MathJax reports unreadable TeX **inside its output** as an `merror` node rather than
by throwing, so catching exceptions is not enough to know a render failed; and the block owns the
failure presentation, so one shape — the source with one restrained diagnostic — covers unsupported
syntax, a parse error and an engine that never loaded. Nothing is ever auto-repaired.

- The renderer is one module with one outcome (`BO_0290_013`, landed 2026-09-23; `lib/mathjax.ts`): given TeX and whether it is display or inline, either markup to draw or a sentence saying why not, so every caller handles failure the same way. Both sites draw through it — the server's render and the browser's, the browser's behind a lazy import — because two renderers that could disagree is the bug where an equation looks one way while it is read and another while it is written.
- **SVG, not CHTML**, is the whole of the answer to the requirement that equations never move the text around them: SVG glyphs are drawn as paths, so nothing waits on a web font and nothing resizes when one lands. `fontCache: "local"` keeps each equation's glyphs inside its own SVG, so a block drawn on its own — in a proposal, in a card — carries everything it needs to be read.
* Every TeX package the engine carries is registered by importing its configuration, because a package *name* alone is not enough: MathJax answers an unregistered one with `Package 'x' not found. Omitted.` rather than by failing, so a missing import would silently narrow what an author may write. Thirty-eight of the forty-two are registered. `require` and `autoload` are left out because they load a package on demand **from a CDN**, which would let a render reach outside the instance — the user's decision of 2026-09-23, and `BO_0163`'s finding, still true of MathJax 4. `noerrors` and `noundefined` are left out because they paper over bad TeX, and the block owns the failure.
- **MathJax reports unreadable TeX inside its output rather than by throwing**, as an `merror` node, so the outcome is checked for the node and not merely for an exception. `BO_0163` paid for this finding on the retired shell; it was confirmed against MathJax 4 before this module was written, by rendering `\frac{1}{` and watching it answer markup rather than raise.
- The memo is what makes typesetting on every read affordable: one source is typeset once, a failure included, and a re-read of an unchanged document typesets nothing. It is bounded at 500 entries because the server process is long-lived and the set of equations it may meet is not.
- Proven in `lib/mathjax.test.ts`: SVG whose glyphs are paths with no font and no address to fetch — the only URLs in the markup are XML namespace names — a display equation set differently from an inline one, unreadable TeX detected without the engine's own error markup reaching a reader, an undefined macro refused, empty source said plainly, `ams`, `physics` and `mhchem` all rendering, `\require` failing rather than fetching, and one source typeset once.
- `EquationBlock` (`views/equation-block.tsx`) is drawn by `BlockRow` where a divider is (`BO_0290_014`, landed 2026-09-23): the equation as the server set it, the number beside it and the caption below. It carries no text editor and no runs; the grip, the drag, the standing, the card and the chip come with the row as they do for a picture and a table.
- **The markup arrives with the block.** `assemble.ts` typesets each equation as it reads it and carries `svg` — or `failure` — on the view, so the row draws markup rather than running a renderer. Two things follow, and both are the point: there is no moment at which the block is a placeholder that something later resizes, and **MathJax never enters the browser's bundle for reading**, because the views import only the *types* from the assembler and a type import is erased at build time. The engine reaches the browser for editing alone.
- The number stands outside the box the equation scrolls in, so a long equation scrolling at phone width never scrolls its own number out of sight; the equation scrolls inside its own block rather than widening the page. The source is the equation's accessible name, so what cannot be seen can still be read.
- TeX the engine could not read draws as the source it was written as, with one restrained sentence, and no equation beside it. The block owns that presentation, so one shape covers unsupported syntax, a parse error and an engine that never ran, and nothing is repaired or guessed (`BO_0163`'s rule).
- Proven in `views/equation-block.test.ts` in the render harness: the set equation drawn with its source as its accessible name and never as its text, its place among the prose held, the number drawn outside the scrolling body and absent when none was asked for, the caption drawn, unreadable TeX drawn as its source with its sentence and no equation beside it, a block with neither markup nor reason saying so, and no text editor on the row. * **An equation stays typeset while its block is edited.** `editor-dom.ts` paints it inside the `contenteditable` as one element the browser must not edit, and reads it back from what the element says it is rather than from what is drawn inside it — walking into one would read an equation's own markup as the sentence's words. ## A Merge Keeps The Words

A block's words were written away by the keystroke after a merge. The reader accepted one block of
an answer, fixated it, added an empty paragraph below it, merged that paragraph back into it, and
the first character they typed became the whole of the block: 652 characters replaced by one word,
with the right base revision, so nothing refused it (`DO_0017`, the document *Qc*, 2026-09-24).

* A save never writes away words the reader did not touch. The editor holds what the block said when
  it opened it, so it is the editor's to know that a reading cannot be the reader's. User decision,
  2026-09-24.
* `Backspace` at the start of an empty block removes that block. It does not merge into the block
  above it: a merge of an empty block changes no words and writes a revision of the block above for
  nothing. User decision, 2026-09-24.
* A reader can take an earlier revision of a block back — the words a merge or a keystroke wrote
  away. User decision, 2026-09-24.
* A fixated block, and a block accepted from a proposal, is edited like any other. Standing belongs
  to reading (Standing Belongs To Reading), so fixate marks what matters to the reader and locks
  nothing. User decision, 2026-09-24.

- The editor drops a reading it cannot be, rather than saving it (`DO_0017_001`). One edit replaces
  one contiguous stretch, so whatever the caret did not touch is still at the start or at the end of
  what comes back: `input$` takes what `runsFrom` answers only when it shares one of those ends, or
  when the block held nothing, or holds nothing now, or the reader had the whole block under the
  caret. A reading sharing neither end of a block nobody selected whole comes from an element holding
  something else, or from one that was never painted; it is dropped, the element is painted again
  from the model, and nothing is sent. At most the keystroke that could not be trusted is lost.
- The rule is deliberately narrow, and narrower than refusing every reading shorter than the model
  less the selection would be: a word deleted, a line deleted and a spellchecker replacing a word all
  take away more than the selection and must not be refused, since a reader whose ordinary editing is
  thrown away is worse off than one who loses a block's words once. `couldBeOneEdit` in `lib/runs.ts`
  is the whole rule, and `runs.test.ts` walks each of those gestures against it.
- The guard is at the reading and not at the save: nothing is refused on the block and nothing is
  confirmed. A dialog in the middle of typing is not a thing this editor does, and a refusal the
  reader cannot clear contradicts Editing And Saving's *leaving is never blocked*. What the save
  would have written was well-formed — the right base revision, so neither the graph nor the kernel
  had any reason to refuse it — which is why nothing downstream can be the one to catch this.
- An empty block is removed, never merged (`DO_0017_002`). `Backspace` at the start of an empty text
  block retires that block and puts the caret at the end of the block above, where a merge left it;
  `Delete` at the end of a block whose next block is empty retires that empty block and leaves the
  caret where it is. Neither sends a merge, so neither writes a revision of the block that keeps its
  words — an empty block joins nothing, and the block above was being written for nothing. A block
  holding words merges as it always did. The removal is the `retire` command, so the block stands in
  the retired list like any other retired row and is restored from there.
- Which block is empty is read from the editor, not from the document in hand: what the block being
  edited holds is `editor.runs`, which the save before the gesture has just made the graph's too,
  and the other block's is the read the gesture takes anyway for the survivor's revision.
- [ ] DO_0017_003 A block's earlier words can be taken back from the bar's block group on the
  subject block, which lists its recent revisions to take one back from. Taking back writes
  forward — a new revision holding the earlier words — so history is never rewritten and the
  take-back is itself takeable back. The provenance read answers the block's history metadata-only
  today ([Block Document Model](./block-document-model.md), `BO_0244_009`), and only a derived
  block's immediately previous words, so this needs a read that carries the words of the revisions it
  offers; what it must not do is carry every revision's content into the document read, which draws
  none of it. Not the retired list, which lists rows that are gone rather than revisions of a row
  that is there, and not a history panel of its own.
* Taking a revision back waits for a change of its own. `DO_0017` closed on the guard and the removal
  (2026-09-24), which are what stop the words being lost; getting earlier words back is a way of
  recovering from a loss rather than a way of preventing one, and it carries a read and a control of
  its own. The task stays here, unclaimed, and is implemented under a change that reaches `ready`.
- Walked on the instance at pin 2487 (`DO_0017_005`, 2026-09-24): `Backspace` in an empty paragraph
  below one holding words removes the empty paragraph, leaves the caret at the end of the words, and
  the words stay through what is typed next. The route the loss came through is gone.
- What the element held at the moment of the loss was never measured, and there is nothing left to
  measure it on: the gesture that reached that moment does not reach it any more. Whatever the
  element held — never painted, or holding another row's words — the reading it produced is what
  `DO_0017_001` drops, so the class is answered at the reading rather than at any one route into it.
  The handover `CA_0045_003` describes, which keeps the outgoing editor mounted until the render that
  mounts its successor, stands as it is: it is what keeps a phone's keyboard from closing between two
  blocks, and nothing has shown it losing anything on its own.
