# Channels

## A Channel Is Data

* A channel is a destination the author's work goes to, created by the author from the library's Channels category: a kind, chosen from the kinds this extension ships ([Channel Kinds](./channel-kinds.md)), and a title. Two channels of one kind are two channels — a development and a production website, two YouTube channels.
* A channel is a `channel` node in the graph carrying its `kind` and its `title`. Its credential and configuration are a party record in the kernel's secret store under the party id `publishing-<channelId>`, never in the graph; its state — `unconfigured`, `configured`, `verified`, `failing` — is read from that record and never stored twice.
* The credential half stays with the settings extension: a channel's row appears in the Channels section of the settings surface, where the author enters the kind's configuration fields and its write-only key, tests, and clears. The channel's own tab links there and holds no credential field. User decision, 2026-09-14; the roster's dynamic half is `CA_0049` in `settings`.
* A channel that has published anything is retired, not deleted, so every log entry keeps naming a destination that existed. A channel nothing was ever published to may be deleted.
- Channels are instance-wide, like connections: there is no owner and no project to scope them to. A project or a per-person scope arrives with the change that introduces those. User decision, 2026-09-14.
- The party id is hyphenated, not `publishing:<channelId>`, because the kernel's store takes a lowercase identifier of letters, digits and hyphens up to 64 characters (`internal/kernel/serve/secrets.go`, `partyPattern`); a channel's UUID fits after the prefix.
- Deleting a channel removes its party record from the kernel's store with the node, so nothing keeps a credential for a destination that is gone.

- `server/roster.ts` is the roster this extension answers through the shell's party roster contribution (`partyRoster` in `contributions.server.ts`): one `PartyDescriptor` per established `channel` node — id `publishing-<channelId>`, `kind: "channel"`, the kind's credential kind (`oauth` for a kind that signs in by a device flow, `apiKey` otherwise; the descriptor carries the kind's `provider`, `paths` and `fixed` configuration, and `createChannel` writes them with the record — `BO_0252_007`), the channel's title as the label, the kind's purpose, its fields and its probe — so the settings surface lists a row for every channel the author made; a channel of a kind the build no longer ships lists with no fields and no probe, so it can still be cleared and deleted. The settings side is in [Channels](../../../../settings/docs/system/channels.md) (`CA_0049_002`).
- [ ] Functional question: does a channel of the `website` kind carry stages — preview, staging, live, each with an address and a key, as studio had — or is each stage its own channel? Recommendation: each is its own channel; a stage is a channel with a different address and the same index.

- `server/channels.ts` holds the operations over the graph and the kernel's store: `createChannel` refuses a kind the registry does not hold (`unknownKind`) and a blank title (`titleRequired`) in words before anything is written, establishes the `channel` node through the bridge's `write` verb with a minted UUID, and then writes the party record under `publishing-<channelId>` with the kind's credential kind, authorization and probe, so the settings row can save, test and clear it; `listChannels` reads every established `channel` and lays each one's state over from the kernel's record, ordered by kind then title; `readChannel` answers the channel, its kind as the browser needs it, its credential facts and the index it declares; `deleteChannel` compares the base revision it was given, retires the node and the index it declared in one script, and removes the party record. A channel of a kind the build no longer ships still lists, by its kind's id, so it can be deleted (`PU_0001_004`).
- `credentialFacts` is where the record and the kind's rule meet: a configuration the kind's fields refuse — through the settings extension's `configurationRefusal`, so one rule serves both surfaces — makes the channel `unconfigured` whatever its key, with the refusal as what it last said. No call here ever reads a secret value; the record answers whether a key is set and its suffix, and the facts carry neither.
- Refusing a delete while the publication log names the channel, and retiring rather than deleting one anything was published to, arrive with the log's first writer (`PU_0003`); until then nothing was ever published to any channel and deleting unconditionally is correct rather than a defect waiting to be found.

## The Channels Category

* The library gains a `Channels` category, contributed by this extension below the shell's own categories: one row per channel — its title, its kind's icon, and its state as the row's badge — opening the channel's tab. A channel never published to reads as that rather than as empty.
* The category's `+` picks a kind and names the channel. It is the category's own control in the Extensions section's idiom — a form under the header with a kind chooser and a title field, *Create* and *Cancel*, nothing stored across reloads — because the header's create control creates at once and can carry no form.
- The category re-reads when a channel tab changes, the rule that keeps Documents current.
- Rows are ordered by kind, then by title, so channels of one kind sit together; the reader decides the order, not the browser.

- The section is `components/channels-section.tsx`, a component section over the reader `channelsListing` in `server/api.ts`, whose answer carries the kinds one can be created as (id, label, fields, whether the kind reads an index) and every channel with its state, so the browser holds no roster. Its controls ride the header's line as `.library-section-actions`: a re-read (Phosphor `arrow-clockwise`, *Read channels again*) and a `+` (*New channel*) toggling the form `data-new-channel-form` — a native select over the kinds by label and a title input, *Create* and *Cancel* — which refuses a blank title in the form without sending, shows the server's refusal by its words, and on success closes, re-reads through `/api/library/publishing/channels` and opens the new channel's tab. A row is a `library-entry` with the kind's icon (`components/icons.tsx`: Phosphor `globe` for `website`, path data unaltered from `@phosphor-icons/core@2.1.1` and inlined the way the shell inlines its own), the title, the state as `library-entry__badge`, and the current marker; the graph not being reachable and no channels each say so (`PU_0001_005`).

## The Channel Tab

* A channel opens as a `publishing:channel` tab. Its view carries a **Credential** section — the state the settings row reports, when it was last tested and what the test said, and a link to that row — and, for a kind whose offer is read from the destination, what the destination declared ([Website](./website.md)). The inspector carries the channel's facts: its kind, its state, when it was last tested.

* An item's tab carries, under *At its channels*, one row per channel whose unit is an item and whose offer takes the item's class — the state and the last publication, the last failed attempt's words — and its inspector *Publish to <channel>* and *Retire at <channel>*, as a deliverable's tab has for the channels that take its shape (`PU_0004`).
- `server/release.ts` carries the item's order beside the deliverable's: `readItemAt` (one row per channel of a kind whose unit is the item and whose fixed offer takes the item's class, with the binding, the state and what the projection would refuse today), `bindItem` (the disclosure, since a fixed offer declares no fields), `publishItem` — the channel usable, the item projected under its title (the gathering deliverable's title with the part's, or the item's label when nothing gathers it), delivered through the kind unless the latest succeeded entry already names the same object, in which case nothing is sent and the new entry carries that entry's id, recorded with the guid as external id — and `retireItem` (`neverPublished`, `alreadyRetired`, the delete at the host, recorded). The routes are `items/[id]/at` (`GET`), `items/[id]/at/[channel]/released`, `items/[id]/at/[channel]/binding` (`PUT`) and `items/[id]/at/[channel]/act` (`POST`, `publish` or `retire`) in `server/work-api.ts`; `deleteItem`'s `liveAtChannel` refusal reads the log as before (`PU_0004_003`).
- `views/item.tsx` draws *At its channels* — one row per channel from `items/[id]/at` with the state, the last publication, the guid once published, the missing list and the last failed attempt — and the inspector's *Publish to <channel>* and *Retire at <channel>*; the inspector's facts follow the rows (the read counts once the rows are in, and the inspector task tracks them — found on the 2026-09-14 walk: the tab said published while the inspector still said never, on both the item and the deliverable tab); a projection refusal is said as the tab's notice in each rule's words, as the deliverable tab does. The deliverable row's missing list names the item and the host channel for a video without a live publication there (`PU_0004_004`).
- The view is `views/channel.tsx`, the `channel` kind's one view, reading `GET /api/x/publishing/channels/[id]` in a visible task. The header carries the kind's icon, the title and the kind's label. The Credential section states the state in words (*Not configured*, *Configured, not yet tested*, *Verified*, *Failing*), each of the kind's fields with its stored value, whether the key is set, and what the record last said; its *Open Settings* control opens the settings tab (`settings:settings`, the instance target) — the row itself is `CA_0049`'s. The inspector's facts are the kind, the state, the last test as a time and the last index read as a time; its actions are *Read the site's index* for a kind that reads one — a refused read is said beside the section's own *Read the site's index* button, where the eye is, not only at the top of the tab — and *Delete channel*, destructive, which sends the revision the view read so a channel that moved is refused rather than deleted, and shows the tab as gone once it is (`PU_0001_006`).

## What A Channel Takes

Under `PU_0003`, requested 2026-09-14: the join between a channel's offer and the author's shapes.

* A channel **takes** shapes: on its tab, *Takes* assigns a shape to a container the site declares and each of the shape's parts to a slot of that container; a part of class `shape` is assigned to a container the nested shape is assigned to. The assignment is refused where the classes differ or the part's constraints cannot meet the slot's — a 16:9 part into a 9:16 slot, a prose part into an image slot — naming the rule, and a slot the site marks `required` with nothing assigned is named as missing. What is assigned is what `reconcileIndex` keeps when the site stops listing a key.
* The channel tab's **Published** section lists the log's entries for the channel, newest first: the record, the act, the outcome and the time.
* A website's video slot **names its host**: on *Takes*, a part assigned to a slot of class `video` may be assigned a `bunny-stream` channel with it — the assignment stands without one, since a video slot is still a website's to declare (user decision, 2026-09-14) — and the website's projection fills the entry's `host` from the item's live publication at that channel, refusing in words — *publish <item> to <channel> first*, or *assign the part with its Bunny Stream channel* — while there is none. Nothing is typed, and the shape stays free of channels. User decision, 2026-09-14 (`PU_0004`).
- The assignment's part entry carries `host` — a channel id, optional for a slot of class `video`, refused for any other class and for a nested part (`hostNotForClass`), refused when the channel does not exist, is not one taking video items or is retired (`notAVideoHost`); `assignPart` in `server/assignments.ts` takes it, `readTakes` answers each entry's host and the instance's video hosts (`videoHosts`: every channel of a kind taking video items, not retired), and the Takes form in `views/channel.tsx` lists an assigned part as *<title> → <slot> at <host>*, offers only the slots of the chosen part's class — the containers for a nested part, and a word when the container has no slot of the class — and, for a video part on a video slot, a select of those hosts beside it, saying to create a Bunny Stream channel under Channels when there is none (found on the 2026-09-14 walk: the slot list once ignored the part, so an image part sat on a video slot with a host select) (`PU_0004_002`).

- `server/assignments.ts` holds what a channel takes as one `assignment` node per channel and shape — the container and the part-to-slot list — related to the channel by `takes` and to the shape by `of`: `takeShape` (a container the index lists and still lists, a shape taken once per channel), `assignPart` (a slot of that container, refused by `assignmentRefusal` in `server/vocabulary.ts` when the classes differ, a part fixed at an aspect the slot does not take, a part allowing a longer duration than the slot, or a part whose formats exclude the slot's; a nested part is assigned to the container the nested shape is taken into, refused while it is not taken), `releasePart`, `dropShape`; `readTakes` answers each shape taken with its parts' slots and the required slots nothing fills; `assignedKeys` is what `server/index-read.ts` hands `reconcileIndex` (`PU_0003_002`).
- The channel tab's *Takes* section is drawn for every website channel: before the site's index has been read it says to read it first, since a shape is taken into a container the site declares; after, it lists each shape taken with its container, each assigned part by its title with its slot and *Unassign*, the required slots left unfilled, a form choosing one of the shape's still unassigned parts by title and a slot by key (`readTakes` hands the shape's parts along as `shapeParts`; a part id is never typed), *Drop* for the shape, and a form taking a shape into a container the index lists; its *Published* section lists the channel's log entries newest first with the record, the act, the outcome, the time and the address; a channel anything was published to offers *Retire channel* in the inspector beside *Delete channel*. A deliverable's tab carries, under *At its channels*, one row per channel that takes its shape — the state and the last publication, the address (an input until the first publication, plain text after), the number for a numbered container, each of the container's fields as the site declares them — an `entry` field as a choice by label among the items filling the parts assigned to its slot (`entries` on the row, by slot key), never an id typed (found on the 2026-09-14 walk: *Card scene names zzze*) — what the projection would refuse today, and the last failed attempt's words — and its inspector one fact per channel with *Publish to <channel>* (offered once there is an address) and *Retire at <channel>* (once published); a publish the projection refuses — nothing attempted, nothing logged — is said as the tab's notice in each rule's words, the same words the row's missing list carries (found on the 2026-09-14 walk: the tab read only the log and stayed silent); a publish that is attempted runs as a process the tab follows ([Publishing](../publishing/publishing.md), A Publish Is A Process, `PU_0009`). An item's rows arrive with the first kind whose unit is an item (`PU_0004`, below) (`PU_0003_006`).
