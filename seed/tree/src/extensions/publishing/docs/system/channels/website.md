# Website

## A Site Declares Its Own Shape

* The `website` kind publishes to a site that implements a contract this extension defines and serves as itself: the site declares what it takes, and Calliopa maps the author's shapes onto it. No site's vocabulary is Calliopa's, and no Calliopa change is needed for a new site. User decision, 2026-09-14, over a kind hard-wired to homepage's `/v1`; `homepage` serves the index of its own shape in its repository first, and `calliopa-video`'s `homepage.ts` is not ported.
* The channel's configuration is the site's **address** (absolute `http` or `https`) and its **key**, presented as `Authorization: Bearer`. The probe is `GET <address>/index` with the key: the smallest authenticated call, and one that changes nothing.
* `GET <address>/index` answers the site's **content index**: `containers` — a page kind with a `key`, a `title`, a route template (starting with `/`, placeholders `{number}`, `{slug}` and `{parent}` only), its `fields` (`line`, `text`, `date`, `flag`, `reference`, `integer`, `entry`, any of them `required`), and whether one exists once, per number or per address; `slots` — each with a `key`, a `title`, a content class (`video`, `image`, `audio`, `prose`, `file`), its constraints (`aspect`, `formats`, a length, cardinality), `required`, and the `container` it belongs to; and `copy` — site-wide fields. Keys are `[a-z0-9-]+` for containers and slots and `[a-z0-9_]+` for fields.
* The index is parsed strictly, and the parser is the refusal rule: a broken index stores nothing and the refusal names the entry and the field. Unknown properties are stripped, so a site may extend its index without breaking a client that does not read the extension.
* A key the next read no longer lists is **retired**, never dropped: it stays while anything is assigned to it, marked as no longer in the index, and is deleted otherwise. A slot whose class changed drops its assignments and says how many.
* Media is declared by SHA-256 (`POST <address>/media` with the media type, size and hash) and uploaded only when the site answers that it does not hold it (`PUT <address>/media/{sha}`); a record naming media the site does not hold is refused by the site and, before that, here. A slot of class `video` never takes bytes: it takes the id the video host answered.
* A container document is written whole by `PUT <address><route>` with its fields and its slot entries, and retired by `DELETE <route>` into a tombstone the site keeps forever. A `2xx` answer that is not the site's JSON is a failure, not a success.
- What the index read stores is the channel's, in the graph, beside the channel: one `contentIndex` node per channel, revised on every read, so what a site declared is readable without asking the site again. The history of what it declared is the node's revisions.
- The route's `{number}` and `{parent}`, the ordered predecessors rule, the singleton container, and the required-slot refusal are the projection's, and arrive with the first publish (`PU_0003`).

- The index document is `version: 1` at its root. A container is `{key, title, description?, route, fields?: [{key, title, type}]}`; a slot is `{key, title, description?, class, container, required?, aspect?, formats?, maxLength?, maxCount?, maxDurationSeconds?}` with `aspect` one of `16:9`, `9:16`, `1:1`, `4:5`, `formats` a list of names and the three limits whole numbers above zero; a copy field is `{key, title, type, group?}` with `type` `line` or `text`. A slot's `container` must be a listed container; keys are unique within their list; a route must start with `/`, name only `{number}`, `{slug}` and `{parent}`, one of `{number}` and `{slug}` at most, and `{parent}` only with one of them. Whether a container exists once, per number or per address is derived from its route (`isSingleton`, `isAddressed`), never declared (`PU_0001_007`, `PU_0007_001`).
- `lib/content-index.ts` is the pure half: `parseContentIndex` answers the index with defaults filled and unknown properties stripped, or every refusal as `{entry, field, message}` — `containers[0].route: route must start with /.` — at once rather than the first; `reconcileIndex` lays a fresh read over what was stored, keeping a vanished key as `inIndex: false` while the set of assigned keys names it and dropping it otherwise, and naming each slot whose class changed. Nothing is assigned before `PU_0003`, so the set handed in today is empty; the rule takes it as an input rather than assuming it. `lib/content-index.test.ts` proves the shape, every refusal, the route rule and the reconciliation without a site (`PU_0001_007`).
- The kernel's broker resolves a request's path against the address the way a browser resolves a link, so an absolute `/index` would replace the address's `/v1` rather than follow it. The kind's own paths — `/index`, `/media`, a container's route — are placed under the address's path first (`underAddress` in `server/kinds/website.ts`, by the transport in `server/release.ts` and by the index read); a path the site answered, its upload path, is host-absolute like homepage's `/v1/media/<sha>` and is sent as it is (`hostPath` on the transport's request). The behaviour suite's stub site serves its API under `/v1` and its address ends with a slash, the way a person writes one, so a path sent at the host's root is refused there. Found on the 2026-09-14 walk: the index read asked the host's root and the site answered 404.
- `server/kinds/website.ts` is the kind: the `address` field checked as `address`, the bearer authorization on `apiKey`, the probe `GET {configuration.address}/index` expecting `200`, `offer: fromDestination`, units `deliverable` and `item`, acts `publish` and `retire`, and no `publish` half yet. `server/index-read.ts` is the read: refused for a kind that declares no index and for a channel still `unconfigured` (naming what to save in Settings); then `GET /index` under the address's own path through the kernel's broker, which adds the key; a status outside `2xx` refused as the site's (`refusedByTheSite`); a body that is not JSON refused as not an index (`notAnIndex`) — a page served for a route that does not exist, or a dev server's error overlay, answers `200` with HTML; the parser's refusals answered as one validation failure naming each; and the reconciled index written as the channel's `contentIndex` — created with its `declares` relation on the first read, revised by `SET` after — answering what was retired, dropped and reclassed (`PU_0001_007`). A revise of the index within 250ms of the last write on it — one read right after another — meets the kernel's per-node floor and is waited out once (`withFloor` in `server/channels.ts`, shared with the delete).
- The channel tab's index section lists what the site declared: each container with its title, its route and its fields, the slots under it with class, `required`, aspect, formats and limits, and the copy fields, each marked *no longer in the index* when a read stopped listing it; a read's changes are shown above the list; *Read the site's index* is the control, in the section and in the inspector, and reads again once one is held (`PU_0001_007`).

## What A Site May Declare Beyond The First Vocabulary

Under `PU_0007`, requested 2026-09-14 when `homepage` (`HP_0023` in its repository) found the vocabulary `PU_0001` parses cannot say four things its `/v1` already takes. Homepage ships its index with the `site` container alone until these land; every widening is additive, so an index a site serves today parses unchanged.

* A container may be **addressed** rather than numbered: its route names `{slug}`, and the record's address comes from its binding at the channel ([Publishing](../publishing/publishing.md), Bindings). `{number}` and `{parent}` stay as they are; a route names one of `{slug}` and `{number}`, never both.
* A container field may **reference** another container: type `reference` with the container it names, taking the address of a record published there; and a field may be an `integer`.
* A slot may declare **fields of its own**, typed as a container's are, filled from the item that fills the slot — a scene's transcript, duration and dimensions and its host id, a still's alt text, prose's title — and the projection refuses an entry whose item lacks what a slot's field requires.
* An image slot may take **one picture in several crops**: `aspects` as a list beside `aspect`, filled from one item's renditions, one per aspect, and refused when a crop is missing.
- The assignment and the projection consume these with the first publish (`PU_0003`); this change gives the index the words, and nothing publishes differently until then.

- A `reference` field takes one address, or a list of addresses when it declares `many: true` — an episode's home serial and card scene are one, its further serials and characters are lists. User decision, 2026-09-14.
- A slot's fields are filled from the item alone; the binding's copy fields stay the per-channel text (`PU_0003`). A label an author wants only at one site is a copy field there, never a second value for a slot field. User decision, 2026-09-14.

- `lib/content-index.ts` carries the widenings: `{slug}` accepted in a route beside `{number}` and `{parent}`, a route naming both `{number}` and `{slug}` refused as *numbered or addressed, not both*, `{parent}` allowed under either, and `isAddressed` derived from the route the way `isSingleton` is (`PU_0007_001`). `FIELD_TYPES` gained `reference` and `integer`; a field carries `container` (a `reference`'s target, checked against the containers the index lists — including one listed after the field's own container, since the keys are collected before any entry is parsed — and refused on any other type) and `many` (refused on any type but `reference`), so a slot's fields and a container's take one set (`PU_0007_002`). A slot carries `fields`, parsed as a container's are with keys unique within the slot, stored on the slot in the `contentIndex` node (`PU_0007_003`), and `aspects`, a list of the four aspects each at most once, refused beside `aspect` on the same slot (`PU_0007_004`). Every refusal names the entry and the field as before.
- The channel tab's index section says how each container is placed — *once*, *numbered*, or *addressed by slug* — shows a reference field as *reference to <container>* or *references to <container>* for a list, lists a slot's own fields under it, and its crops beside its class (`PU_0007_005`).
- `lib/content-index.test.ts` proves each widening and each refusal over plain values, and `tests/behavior/channels.test.ts` reads, from the stub site through the broker, an index declaring an addressed container with one and many references and an integer, a slot with its own field, and a slot with three crops, and reads every new property back from the stored index (`PU_0007_006`).

## Naming A Record's Own Slot Entries

Under `PU_0008`, requested 2026-09-14 when `homepage` (`HP_0024` in its repository) found no word for a field naming one of the record's own slot entries — an episode's card scene, the site document's flagship scene. A `line` carrying an entry's id says nothing a client can check, and an id refused only at the site's boundary at publish time is the `ST_0050` lesson; homepage leaves both undeclared until this lands.

* A field may name **one of the record's own slot entries**: type `entry`, carrying `slot` — a slot of the same container — and `many` for a list. Its value is the id of an entry in that slot of the same record, and a client checks it before anything is sent: the entry must exist in the deliverable being published.
* An entry's identity is the item's own, stable across re-exports: a container document sends each slot entry with the id of the item that fills the slot, and the site keeps that id as the entry's. This is the wire shape's rule, decided here so the field type has something to point at, and carried into the container document `PU_0003` defines.
- The widening is additive: an index declaring no `entry` field parses unchanged.

- A field of any type may be `required` by the site, an additive property with `false` as its default; the projection refuses a required field left empty before anything is sent. User decision, 2026-09-14.

- `lib/content-index.ts` carries the field: `FIELD_TYPES` gained `entry`; a field of that type carries `slot`, checked against the slots the index lists under the field's own container — collected before any entry is parsed, as the container keys are, so a slot listed after the field counts — and refused naming the entry when it is not one or belongs to another container; `slot` is refused on any other type, `many` allowed on `entry` as on `reference`, and `required` parsed on every field. A slot's own fields take the same rule against their slot's container (`PU_0008_001`). The channel tab shows an `entry` field as *entry of <slot>* or *entries of <slot>* for a list, and *required* beside any field that is (`PU_0008_002`). `lib/content-index.test.ts` proves the field before and after its slot, the cross-container refusal, a missing slot, `slot` on another type, `required` and `many`; `tests/behavior/channels.test.ts` reads an index declaring a required `entry` field from the stub site through the broker and reads it back from the stored index (`PU_0008_003`). The release-notes line is under *Added* in `calliopa-bootstrap`'s `docs/release-notes/unreleased.md` (`PU_0008_004`).

## The Container Document

Under `PU_0003`, requested 2026-09-14. The one thing this extension sends a site that the site has to accept: proposed here so `homepage` (`HP_0024` in its repository, which waits on it) answers on the same text.

* A container document is written whole by `PUT <address><route>`, the route's placeholders filled from the record's binding, as JSON: `fields` keyed by the container's field keys, each typed as the index declares it — `line` and `text` a string, `date` ISO-8601, `flag` a boolean, `integer` a number, `reference` the address of a record published at the site or a list when `many`, `entry` the id of an entry in the named slot of this document or a list — and `slots` keyed by slot key, each a list of entries.
* An entry carries `id` — the id of the item filling the part, stable across re-exports — its slot `fields` from the item's facts alone, and exactly one of `media` (one object's SHA-256, for an image, audio or file slot), `crops` (one object per aspect, for an image slot with `aspects`), `host` (the video's id at the channel's video host; never bytes), or `document` (the prose body in the shell's block vocabulary — the five text roles, the four marks, links and the divider — as the site accepts it).
* Every object the document names is declared before the document is written — `POST <address>/media` with the media type, size and hash answers whether the site holds it and, when not, where to `PUT` the bytes — so a retry sends nothing the site already has.
* The site answers JSON: the stored record on `200` or `201`, or the envelope `{error: {code, message, field?, rule?}}`; anything else is a failure, never a success. `DELETE <address><route>` retires the record, a body naming `supersededBy` — an address at the same site — when the author writes one. A singleton container's document is the same shape at its fixed route.

- `server/kinds/website.ts` carries the kind's `publish` half. `projectContainer` is pure: over a submission — the deliverable read, its binding, the container and its slots from the channel's index, the assignment, the items it gathers with their exports and body documents, the gathering deliverables' addresses at the channel with whether each is published, and the video host ids — it answers the container document (the route filled from the binding, the fields, the slot entries, the objects to declare) or every rule it breaks: `addressRequired`, `numberRequired`, `fieldRequired`, `slotRequired`, `tooMany`, `slotFieldRequired`, `cropMissing`, `exportMissing`, `noVideoHost` (every video until `PU_0004` fills `hosts`; after it, a video whose item has no live publication at the slot's host channel, naming both), `proseBodyMissing`, `unsupportedBlock`, `entryUnknown`, `referenceUnpublished`, `slotGone`. A reference is filled from the gathering deliverables first and from the binding otherwise; a slot's field from the item alone (label or title, alt, transcript, duration, width, height, synthetic). `deliverContainer` is the transport: every object declared by `POST /media` with exactly `mimeType`, `bytes` and `sha256` — homepage's declaration is a strict object, and a fourth key is refused as unknown (found on the 2026-09-14 walk; the stub site is as strict) — the absent ones uploaded to the path the site answered, then `PUT` on the route; a `2xx` that is not JSON is a failure, and a site's own envelope comes back in its words. `retireContainer` is `DELETE` on the route with the successor when the author wrote one. `server/kinds/website.test.ts` proves the document's shape and every refusal over records, without a site (`PU_0003_004`).
- `submissionFor` builds `hosts` from the log: for every video item the deliverable gathers, the state at the channel its part's assignment names as host — `stateAt`, which now carries `externalId` — and the id of its live publication there; `hostNames` carries the host channel's title, so `noVideoHost` says *Scene takes the id of a video at Bunny, and Main is not published there yet; publish it to Bunny first*, or, for a part assigned with no host, that the part must be assigned with its Bunny Stream channel. `tests/behavior/publish.test.ts` proves the refusal while the video is retired at the host and the document's entry carrying the guid once it is published there (`PU_0004_005`).
