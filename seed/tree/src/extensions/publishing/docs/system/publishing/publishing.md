# Publishing

## Three Layers

* The model has three layers and they stay separate. **Work** — what exists: shapes, deliverables, items, renditions, documents ([Shapes And Deliverables](../shapes/shapes-and-deliverables.md)). **Distribution** — what may go where: the channels, what each takes, the assignment of parts onto slots, and what a record is worth at a channel. **Publication log** — what actually happened, append-only.
* An item never carries a published flag or a channel list. Where it went is the log's answer; where it may go is the distribution layer's.
- The distribution layer's selector — windows, budgets, TTLs, reserved slots — stays empty by design: it needs a clock and unattended action, which the platform does not have and the human-act rule forbids. The log and the channel model are built so it has somewhere to land.

## Every Publication Is A Human Act

* The system prepares and a person releases, for every channel. Nothing publishes unattended. An agent may prepare — copy, a selection, a framing — and propose it; nothing an agent produces reaches a platform on its own.

## A Publish Is A Process

Under `PU_0009`, requested 2026-09-14 on the `PU_0004` walk: *a process indicating that this is running, and the result reported.*

* Every publish and every retirement is a process in the shell's registry ([Processes](../../../../../docs/system/workspace/processes.md) in `ui.shell`, `CA_0050`): pressing *Publish to <channel>* or *Retire at <channel>* creates one in the reader's workspace — *Publish E1 to Calliopa.com*, *Retire Main at Bunny* — affecting the deliverable or item on its tab kind, moved to `running` before the first byte leaves and through the order's steps in words — *declaring 3 objects*, *uploading 2 of 3*, *writing the document*, *uploading to Bunny* — and to `completed` with the outcome as its last step, or `failed` with the delivery's words as its error. A projection refusal creates no process: nothing was attempted, and the tab says the rules at once.
* The act answers at once with the process it started; the order runs on in the server. The tab follows the process, not the request: the pressed act is withheld while its process runs and offered again when it ends, and the rows at the channels are read again then.
* The process is the feedback, the log is the record: a process that ended names the log entry's id in its last step, and nothing about the outcome is stored twice.
- `startAct` in `server/release.ts` takes the record kind and id, the channel, the act and the `workspaceId`: a publish is projected first and its rules answered with no process when it is refused; otherwise the process is created through the shell's `createProcess` — *Publish E1 to Calliopa.com*, *Retire Main at Bunny*, the record as the affected item under `publishing:deliverable` or `publishing:item` — answered as `{ processId }`, and the order runs detached, moved to `running` at *starting* and at each step the transport reports through the `Progress` callback threaded into `deliverContainer` (*declaring N objects*, *uploading k of N*, *writing <route>*), `retireContainer`'s caller (*retiring <route>*), `deliverVideo` (*creating <title> at Bunny*, *uploading N MB to Bunny*) and `retireItem` (*retiring <guid> at Bunny*); it ends `completed` with *landed at <address> · entry <id>* (or *retired at*), or `failed` with the delivery's words and, when an entry was logged, *entry <id>* as the step. `publishDeliverable`, `retireDeliverable`, `publishItem` and `retireItem` take the callback and are silent without it. The act routes in `server/work-api.ts` run the act as a process when the body carries `workspaceId` and to its outcome otherwise, which is what the suites and the gates call (`PU_0009_001`).
- `views/deliverable.tsx` and `views/item.tsx` send the bridge's `workspaceId` with the act, hold the process by channel (`running`, `steps`), withhold that channel's acts from the inspector while it runs and show *Running: <step>* as the channel's fact, poll `/api/processes/<id>` every two seconds until it ends, then read the rows again and say a failure in the process's words as the notice; a projection refusal is said at once as before (`PU_0009_002`).
- `tests/behavior/publish.test.ts` proves the act as a process against the kernel's state record: a publish started with a workspace creates *Publish E1 to Stub site* affecting the deliverable and ends `completed` naming the address and the entry; the site answering HTML ends it `failed` with the site's words; a refused projection answers the rules and creates none; an item's publish to the stub Bunny ends `completed` as *Publish Main to Stub Bunny* (`PU_0009_003`).
- The release-notes line is under *Added* in `calliopa-bootstrap`'s `docs/release-notes/unreleased.md` (`PU_0009_004`).

## Bindings

* A **binding** is what one deliverable or item is worth at one channel: its address there (a slug, freely editable until the record has been published at that channel and a permanent citation target afterwards), the per-channel **copy** — title, caption, description, tags, the thumbnail choice — written by the author or proposed by an agent as fields on the binding, and the disclosure the platform's control needs. Observed facts are never stored on a binding; they are read from the log. User decision, 2026-09-14: copy is fields on the binding, revisioned and reviewable; prose parts stay documents.

- A binding's address is the `{slug}` a route names or the `{number}` a numbered container takes; its fields are the values of the container's fields the site declares, typed as the index declares them; an unpublished record's binding may be revised freely, and a published one's address is refused with the remedy — retire there, publish at the new address.

- A reference field is filled from the deliverable that gathers this one, at its address on the channel, when that deliverable's shape is assigned to the referenced container — and refused while that deliverable is not published there — and from an address the author typed on the binding otherwise; a `many` reference lists every gatherer. User decision, 2026-09-14.
- A numbered container's `{number}` is typed on the binding, never derived from the gathering order, so a reorder moves no published address. User decision, 2026-09-14.
- `server/bindings.ts` keeps a binding keyed by record and channel — a read before the write, since the graph validates one node at a time: `writeBinding` creates the first and revises a later one, an omitted value left as it stands and an explicit null cleared; the address is refused in shape (`addressShape`) and, once the record was ever published at the channel, refused to change (`addressSettled`, naming the remedy); the number is a whole number from 1; each field's value is checked against the container's field type by `fieldValueRefusal` in `server/vocabulary.ts` (`fieldValue`), a field the container does not declare refused (`unknownField`); a required field left empty is the projection's refusal, so a half-written binding can be saved. `readBinding` answers an empty binding that says the address may still change when none was written (`PU_0003_003`).

## The Publication Log

* The log is append-only: an entry per attempt naming the deliverable, the item and rendition where one was sent, the channel, the act, the outcome, the platform's id and address, and the time. An entry is a node created as an established revision and never revised, retired or removed.
* A record's state at a channel — never published, published, retired — is derived from its entries, never stored beside them. A republication is a new entry. A failure after a success leaves the state at what last succeeded.
* A projection refusal is not a failed publication: nothing was attempted, and an entry saying otherwise would be a false record. A delivery failure is logged, because something was.
* An object a successful entry names is permanent: a rendition's bytes are a CCGW blob the rendition references, and a referenced blob is never removed.
* Deleting a record that is live at any channel is refused, naming each channel; retiring it there first is its own act.

- A release that fails part-way — the site holds media and refuses the document — is logged as failed; the author's next press runs the whole order again, every object declared and only the absent ones sent, and nothing is undone at the site. User decision, 2026-09-14.
- The types are declared as this extension's members and seeded in the kernel harness fixture: `assignment` (`channel`, `shape`, `container`, `parts` as the part-to-slot list, each entry with `host` for a video slot after `PU_0004`), `binding` (`recordId`, `recordKind` one of `deliverable`, `item`; `channel`; `fields`; optional `address`, `number`, `disclosure`), `release` (`recordId`, `recordKind`, `recordTitle`, `channel`, `act` one of `publish`, `retire`; `outcome` one of `succeeded`, `failed`; `objectIds`; `at`; optional `externalId`, `externalAddress`, `detail`); relations `takes` (channel to assignment) and `of` (assignment to shape). A binding and a release carry their keys as properties (`PU_0003_001`).
- `server/release.ts` is the order — gather (`submissionFor`: the channel usable, its index read, the shape taken, the container still listed, the binding, every item with its exports and body document, the gathering deliverables' addresses and states), project, deliver, record — behind `publishDeliverable`, `retireDeliverable`, `readAt` (one row per channel that takes the deliverable's shape, with what the projection would refuse today) and `bindDeliverable`. `server/releases.ts` is the log: `recordSuccess` and `recordFailure` the only writes, each a `release` node created as an established revision and never revised; `stateAt` derives never, published or retired from the entries with the first and last publication, the address and the last attempt; `entriesForRecord`, `entriesForChannel`, `liveAt` and `channelWasPublishedTo` are the reads. A projection refusal is answered with `released: false` and no entry; a delivery failure is an entry. `deleteDeliverable` and `deleteItem` refuse while the record is live at any channel (`liveAtChannel`); `deleteChannel` refuses once anything was published to it (`channelPublishedTo`, *retire it instead*), and `retireChannel` keeps the node marked `retired` — an undeclared property Validation tolerates as open content — and removes the party record (`PU_0003_005`).

## Rules Every Kind Follows

* A `2xx` that is not the platform's own answer is a failure. A page served for a route that does not exist, or an error overlay while a site restarts, answers `200` with HTML; a publish that read that as success would record a publication nobody performed.
* Refuse, never skip: a slot that cannot be filled stops the publish rather than thinning it, and a projection reports every refusal at once rather than the first.
* Never silently drop authored data: a platform that takes fewer of something than the author wrote is a refusal, not a truncation.
* A gate that reaches a real destination refuses with a non-zero exit when its channel is unconfigured rather than skipping; a gate that skipped would report proof nobody produced.
- The first of these is already applied where this extension first reaches a destination: a website answering something that is not JSON where its index should be is refused as not an index, and what was stored stands ([Website](../channels/website.md)).
