# Focused Work

## Purpose

- This document is the authoritative description of focused work as a capability of the shell: opening a block of a view's target as its own work root, coming back along the route, and what a view draws for it.
- `CA_0065_FEAT_focused-work-is-a-shell-capability` is the originating change. `CA_0047` built the capability; this change makes it the frame's.
- [Workspace View Types](./view-types.md) owns the view contract this extends. [Tabs](./tabs.md) owns the route, the tabs focused work opens and the workspace record, which are already the shell's and do not move.
- What each view draws for it is its own: `documents`' [Block Editor](../documents/block-editor.md), *Focused Work*, has the control's place on the block, the face's line and the pinch.

## What Focused Work Is

* Any block can be opened as focused work. The focused block becomes the current work root: the interface then shows the child's own words and work, and returning to the containing work copies nothing: the parent renders the child's face on the block, which a document child wears as the first words of the blocks it holds, one line each under the block, and as its title while it holds none with words (`BO_0243`, the material's §8; `CA_0047`; `BO_0324`; the lines are `calliopa-bootstrap`'s `BO_0349_019`, user decision from the walk, 2026-10-05, after a nest showed the target's own words where the dragged block had gone).
* The affordance is discoverable. A focused block's expansion ends with *Open as focused work →*, and *Back* or a crumb returns to the containing work. No pinch opens focused work or goes back: on touch, a pinch asks for work on the block instead (user decision, 2026-09-30, `BO_0322_Q1`, `BO_0322_Q11`; `documents`' [Block Editor](../../../src/extensions/documents/docs/system/documents/block-editor.md#a-pinch-deepens-or-gathers)). The reader never thinks *create a new document*. No gesture is the only path (the material's §42, §43).
* Navigation is route, not ontology: the route above the intent is the path by which the reader arrived and nothing else.
* Work drifts naturally: a block becomes independently focusable when it has accumulated enough of its own, and the reader simply opens it. No conceptual migration is asked of them.
- These four are the shell's since `CA_0065_006` (2026-09-23), unchanged in their words: they say what focused work is, which is the frame's to say now that the capability is.

## Why It Is The Shell's

- The whole effect of opening a block as focused work is on the frame: a tab is opened or made active, it is given a route, a block is landed on. Those are three things the shell owns and no view may do for itself ([View Types](./view-types.md), View Contract).
- Every view over every target kind has the same need — open one part of what I am reading as its own root, and come back. Offered by an extension, it reaches one view and costs any other view two imports.

## What Belongs Where

* **Focused work is a capability of the shell.** The child's creation, the `focuses` edge, the reads that answer a block's child and a target's faces, the tab the child opens in, the route and the landing are the frame's, offered to every view. User decision, 2026-09-23.
* **The shell asks the target's own extension for the child.** `document`, `text` and `contains` are `documents`' vocabulary and only `focuses` is the shell's, so the shell never writes another extension's types: the extension contributing a target kind contributes how a child of that kind is made, and the capability is general from the start rather than document-shaped. User decision, 2026-09-23, over the shell writing `documents`' vocabulary directly.
* **The shell contributes the control, the view renders it.** *Open as focused work* is a shell-contributed action in the view's own block row, in the named-action vocabulary the bar and the inspector already share, so every install has one way in and no extension can withhold it. The shell still draws nothing inside a view's content: the view declares the slot and renders the action through `ActionControl`. User decision, 2026-09-23.
* **The parent's face is the view's to draw, from a read the shell answers.** A block whose child holds a synthesis wears it as a muted line; what that line looks like is the view's, and the faces come from the shell as `focus` and `reveal` already do. User decision, 2026-09-23.
- What stays the view's besides: the gesture that means it — `documents`' pinch adapter over the shell's `src/lib/pinch.ts` — where the route line renders until `CA_0048` moves it into the frame, and what landing on a block means.
- Nothing about the model changes. A focused work is still a document that `focuses` a block, a block is focused by at most one document, the child is parentless in the library's sense, retiring a focused block is still refused, and the route stays navigation only, with no governance, ownership or containment meaning ([Block Document Model](../documents/block-document-model.md)).

## The Child Contribution

- An extension contributing a target kind contributes how a child of that kind is made and read (`CA_0065_001`, landed 2026-09-23): `FocusedWorkContribution` on `ServerContributions`, keyed by the bare kind, carrying `childType` — the node type a child is, which the shell reads the `focuses` edge to — `plan`, which answers the child a block would open as, and `faces`, which answers what each child says for itself. A target kind that contributes none opens no focused work, and the shell's control is not drawn for it.
- The plan is statements, not a write (`src/contract.ts`, `ChildPlan`): the child's identity, its title, the statements creating it and the parameters they read. The extension commits nothing; the shell folds the plan into its own script so the child and the edge are one write. Which of its blocks may be opened, and the refusal in words when one may not, are the extension's — `documents` refuses a block that is not in the target and a block that is not `text`.
- `buildServerRegistry` keys the contributions by the qualified kind, as a tab's kind is (`registry.ts`, `focusedWork`), so the shell reaches the contribution from the target it already holds; `focusedWorkFor` in `src/server/registry.ts` is the lookup. No collision is possible and none is refused: a kind is already namespaced by the extension contributing it. `src/registry.test.ts` proves the keying and that a kind contributing none answers nothing.

## The Capability

- The capability is `src/server/focused-work.ts` (`CA_0065_002`, landed 2026-09-23): `FOCUSES`, `childrenOf` — the rooted read over `focuses` seeded at the blocks — `facesOf`, `openFocusedWork` and `focusOf`, each taking the qualified target kind and reaching the contribution through it. A kind that contributes none is refused `focusedWorkUnsupported` in words. What it answers no longer names a document: `FocusedChild` is `itemId`, `title` and `face`, and `OpenedFocusedWork` is `itemId`.
- The child and the edge are one write. `focusScript` is pure — the extension's statements, then `RELATE fwChild -[f:focuses]-> fwBlock`, with the plan's parameters under the shell's two refs — so a child never stands without the edge that makes it focused work, and the composition is proven without a graph (`src/server/focused-work.test.ts`). The shell's `fwChild` and `fwBlock` are written last, so an extension that happened to use those names cannot redirect the edge.
- The contribution is resolved when it is needed, not when the module loads. Technical decision at implementation, 2026-09-23: the server registry imports every extension's server half, so a static import would close a cycle through any extension module that opens focused work — `documents`' own writes do — and leave one extension's contributions undefined while the registry was still being built.
- The frame's own endpoint (`CA_0065_003`, landed 2026-09-23): `GET /api/focused-work/<item>?kind=<qualified kind>` answers the face of every block of the target that has focused work, and `POST` with `{ kind, blockId }` opens one block or answers the child that already focuses it. A view asks through the bridge and never through the extension that owns the target's vocabulary; the kind names which contribution answers. `documents`' own `openFocusedWork` command and its `focused` route are gone, and so is the entry `calliopa-refine` served with `documents`' handler.
- The bridge carries three things (`CA_0065_003`, `CA_0065_004`, `CA_0065_005`): `pressBlockControl$`, which acts and answers a refusal in words or `null`; `blockControls$`, which answers what the frame offers on one block; and `faces$`, which answers what a target's blocks have. The view names its target and its block and never a kind — the shell reads the kind from the tab the target is open in (`kindOf` in `src/lib/tabs.ts`), so a vocabulary stays out of a view's reach. *Back* and the crumbs reach the parent through `openAlongRoute$` ([View Types](./view-types.md), `CA_0073_002`).
- The shell keeps the faces a view read, by target, so a control's words can say whether the block already has a child without a read per block. `documents` starts the batched faces read as its document enters reading presentation, after its first render, and draws each face as the answer arrives; the page and block controls never wait for it (`DO_0029_001`, `DO_0001`). The bridge and its target-level cache stay the same.
- The contract now carries one thing the other way (`CA_0065_004`, landed 2026-09-23). `BlockControl` is what the shell offers on a block — an id, the words and an icon, and no handler: the view wraps it in a `ViewAction` whose `run$` calls `pressBlockControl$` and renders it through `ActionControl`, so the shell mints no QRL at runtime and the one named-action vocabulary still covers every surface. `ActionSurface` gained `"block"` beside `"inspector"` and `"bar"`, and a control drawn there carries `data-block-action`.
* A view says where the shell's controls are drawn and nothing else. It may not change a control's words, its order among the shell's, or whether it is offered: a capability the frame provides is not an extension's to withhold, which is the whole reason focused work moved. User decision, 2026-09-23.
- The faces are a call, not a store (`CA_0065_005`, landed 2026-09-23): `faces$(itemId)` answers each of the target's blocks that has a child, with the child's identity, its title and its own words when it holds any. `focus` and `reveal` are one-shot signals the shell writes; what a target's blocks have opened is data a view asks for when it first needs it, so a call is the shape and the read stays off the document read. Technical decision at implementation, revising the store this task first named.

## What Moves, And From Where

- The move landed whole (`CA_0065_006`, 2026-09-23): `documents` draws the control, the face and the pinch and contributes the child; and the four fixed product lines stand above, unchanged, with `documents`' *Focused Work* keeping what it itself draws.
- Technical decision, 2026-09-23: the behaviour suite moves to the tree root's `tests/behavior/` beside the shell's others. The kernel harness runs `vitest run --project behavior` over the whole project rather than a named list (`internal/kernel/serve/shell_documents_verification_test.go`), so no fixed-layer change follows and `CA_0065` has no `BO` half.
- Technical decision, 2026-09-23: the pinch adapter stays each view's. `src/lib/pinch.ts` is already the shell's and pure; a view that wants the gesture installs the adapter and calls `openFocusedWork$`, as `documents` does today.

## Nesting A Dragged Block

* A block dropped onto another block — on the middle of its height, not between two rows — nests: the block it lands on becomes focused work, as *Open as focused work* would make it, or keeps the focused work it has, and the dragged block moves into it after its last block. The reader stays where they are, and the target block wears its focused work's face at once. A block that has focused work of its own keeps it when it is dragged: the work travels with it. User decision, 2026-09-29 (`CA_0072`). Focused work a nest opens is made with no block of its own, so the dragged block is its first and nothing empty stands above it. User decision, 2026-10-05 (`calliopa-bootstrap`'s `BO_0349_019`).
- A blank child (`BO_0349_019`, landed 2026-10-05): the frame's `POST /api/focused-work/<item>` takes `blank: true`, which `openFocusedWork` hands the contribution's `plan` as `blank`, and `documents` then plans the document alone, with no first block. `focusedChild$` always asks for it, since only a nest calls it; *Open as focused work* and the face do not, so a child opened to write in still starts with a block. A face carries `lines` beside `face` (`ChildFace`, `FocusedChild`): the first words of the child's blocks that have any, which `documents` answers and draws. Proven in `documents`' `tests/behavior/focus.test.ts` (a blank child holding the moved block alone, and the face listing its words) and `views/focused-work.test.ts`.
- The bridge answers a block's child without leaving the document (`CA_0072_005`): `focusedChild$(itemId, blockId)` asks the frame's `POST /api/focused-work/<item>` — which opens the block's focused work or answers the child it has, through the target kind's contribution, exactly as the control does (`openChild$`) — and answers `{ itemId }`, or `{ refusal }` in words, without retargeting the tab or pushing the route. It then reads the target's faces again (`readFaces$`, which `faces$` is too), so the view draws the new face. The view moves the block itself, in its own vocabulary ([Block Editor](../documents/block-editor.md), Dragging Into Blocks And Other Documents). `documents`' render harness carries the same call over the same routes and records it; `views/nesting.test.ts` proves a nest asking for the child and no retarget.

## Focused Work In Its Own Tab

* Opening a block as focused work always opens the child in a tab of its own — by the control and by a press on the parent's face alike (the pinch left it under `BO_0322`). The parent's tab stays on the parent, with its scroll, its focused block and its unsaved state as they were, and the reader moves between the two by selecting a tab. User decision, 2026-09-30 (`CA_0073`).
* The child's tab opens right after the parent's tab and becomes active. When the child is already open in a tab, that tab becomes active instead of a second one opening. User decision, 2026-09-30 (`CA_0073`).
* *Back* and a crumb make the named document's tab active and land on the block that was opened from it; when that document is open in no tab, it opens in a tab of its own beside the one the reader pressed in. The tab pressed in stays open. User decision, 2026-09-30 (`CA_0073`).
* The child's tab keeps the route line, unchanged: it is the path the reader came by and the one visible way up on a desktop. Tabs already stored with a route longer than one are not migrated; a crumb in them behaves as above. No control replaces the current tab with the child. User decision, 2026-09-30 (`CA_0073`).
- The child opens in its own tab (`CA_0073_001`, landed 2026-09-30): `pressBlockControl$` in `src/components/shell/shell.tsx` opens or finds the child through the kind's contribution, as before, builds its route — the parent tab's route with the opened block on the parent's entry and the child last — and hands it to the shell's `openAlongRoute$`, which opens the child's tab right after the parent's, or makes a tab already showing the child active with the route it has, and lands on nothing ([Tabs](./tabs.md)). The parent's tab is left as it is. The pinch presses the same control, so it opens the same tab. `documents`' `views/focused-work.test.ts` proves the child's tab opened after the parent's with the parent's tab unchanged.
- Focused work in its own tab was walked by the user on a desktop and a phone on the served build at pin 3284, 2026-09-30 (`CA_0073_004`), and works. The walk covered opening by the control, the face and the pinch; switching between the parent's and the child's tabs, each found as it was left; *Back*, a crumb and the inward pinch with the parent's tab open and closed; a stored tab with a route of two; and the phone's *Minimum* header.
