# Layout

## Desktop Layout

* Header: project identity, horizontally scrollable tabs, process status indicator, layout controls, settings, light/dark toggle, and the signed-in person's menu at the very right.
* The header is always one line, as tall as the laurel and the *Calliopa* wordmark, on both form factors and whatever it holds. On a phone in the header's *Full* mode the tab strip below it is the one exception (Mobile Layout, Header Modes).
* Left drawer: hierarchical navigation for projects, stories, acts, scenes, characters, media, and saved views.
* Center workspace: whatever the active tab represents — script editor, storyboard, timeline, scene board, or asset view.
* Right drawer: the inspector — the active view's contributed facts and actions, a selected process's detail in their place, and the *Execution* list of what is running and what has run. It is not a second permanent navigation area.
* Each drawer is hidden, shown with its icon column alone (the small mode), or shown with one icon's content (Panels As Activity Bars). User decision, 2026-09-18 (`CA_0056_001`).

- The chrome is quiet by default. Layout controls, the theme toggle, and the drawer close controls are transparent icon buttons that gain a surface and border on hover, not bordered form buttons.
- Settings is Phosphor `gear`, and the two layout controls are `sidebar-simple`, the right drawer's mirrored by a CSS transform so the path stays unaltered. Their names are unchanged (`CA_0041_002`).
- The one loud element in the header is the owner's update hint (`BO_0223_013`, 2026-09-09): *Update available: <version>*, bordered in the accent, shown only while the session is the owner's, the build holds the settings extension's `update` kind, the release check is on and GitHub's release list holds a release above the installed one. The check runs in the owner's browser once per session (`src/lib/releases.ts`, remembered in `sessionStorage`) and nowhere in the stack; the kernel's session answer says who the owner is (`src/server/session.ts`, `owner`), and the installed release comes from the settings extension's update read. Off, unreachable, or nothing newer all mean no hint and no error. The hint opens the Update tab.
- The signed-in person is `PersonMenu` (`src/components/shell/header-disclosure.tsx`), the last control on the header's line: a Phosphor `user` button whatever the person's class, named `Signed in as <name>. Account`, whose panel opens under it at its right edge with the name, the role in the header's words (`may establish` for a human, `proposes` otherwise) and *Sign out*, which posts `/__kernel/session/sign-out` and then goes to the kernel's sign-in page. `data-identity` and `data-identity-class` stay on its wrapper (`CA_0041_003`).
- The person's menu and the licence warning share `HeaderDisclosure`, a disclosure rather than an ARIA menu: the button carries `aria-expanded` and `aria-controls`, not `aria-haspopup`, because the panel says who is signed in and what the licence says, and a `role="menu"` may hold nothing but its items. Focus stays on the button, and Tab reaches the panel. A press outside (a `document:` listener, as the agent menu has), Escape anywhere in it, or a second press on the button closes it, and Escape returns focus to the button. The panel sits at z-index 30: above the workspace and the phone's sheets, below messages and the drag preview. `header.test.ts` presses the button, reads the name, the role and *Sign out*, closes the panel by a second press and by Escape from the button and from inside the panel, and signs out. The press outside is the walk-through's, since the harness dispatches no `document:` listener (`CA_0041_003`).
- The licence warning is `LicenceWarning`: a Phosphor `warning` button in the `error` colour on both form factors, named by the licensor's text, whose panel shows the text. It keeps `data-licence-warning` and its `role="status"` wrapper, and renders for as long as `src/lib/licence.ts` says the warning is live. The render harness proves it, since this instance's licence is in force (`CA_0041_004`).
- The workspace region renders no heading and no view-name tag. A tag naming the tool is chrome the tab strip already supplies, and the headline belongs to the view that owns the content and can edit it, so a title is rendered once by that view. The region names itself directly, keeping its landmark (`CA_0013_001`).
- The library arrives shown with its first icon, and the inspector arrives hidden, in a workspace that never chose (`CA_0056_004`).
- The left drawer holds one top-level category, `Documents`, listing the parentless documents [Block Document Model](../documents/block-document-model.md) answers, each entry naming its document by title. It stands under the *Docs* icon, the library's first, so the library is visible without being opened.
- A document's row in the `Documents` category carries a glyph for its derived state (`BO_0248_012`; `LibraryItem.glyph`, the documents reader over `GET documents/states`): Phosphor `warning-circle` for *under pressure*, `warning` for *needs review*, named so; a document with neither carries nothing; the section re-reads after a system run ends (the shell's task on the proposed signal).
- Activating an entry opens that document in a tab. A tab already showing the document is revealed rather than opened a second time, because a tab is found by its target rather than by its identity.
- The entry for the active tab's document reads as selected, carrying a marker and `aria-current` rather than colour alone.
- With no document in the graph the category shows an empty-state line under its heading, so the region reads as a place that holds things rather than as something that failed to load.
- The category header carries a Phosphor `plus` icon button that creates a document and opens it in its own tab, in the chrome idiom above and with its own accessible name. It is the only way to create a document; the header offers none.
- The category is collapsible, and its collapsed state is stored in the workspace record, so it follows the workspace across reloads and devices.
- Documents live in the graph rather than in a workspace, so every workspace lists every document. Scoping the library to a project or a workspace arrives with the change that introduces those.
- The rendered listing is rebuilt rather than reconciled: a read raises a counter the list is keyed by. Entries move and are renamed in the same read, and a keyed diff over that left stale names on reused rows. A tab is keyed by its identity and its title for the same reason, so a renamed target gives its tab a new label.
- A rename re-reads the listing rather than re-sorting it in the browser. Title order is the server's, so the drawer cannot disagree with what the next read would say.
- The `Extensions` category is a tree: under each extension row, indented, its change documents — its `docs/changes/` members — each the icon that stands for the status its `Status:` line carries and the file's name, opening the change in the extension view by its member path so one already open is revealed, ordered by that path, which is identifier order for every change the protocol names. The row carries no create control: a change document is written by an agent working from a checkout, in the proposal that carries the code, and there is no *Other* group, because a member is always under the extension whose subtree holds it. The category re-reads when an extension's members change. `BO_0254_009`
- `readExtensionDocument` reads an extension's changes from its members alone (`BO_0254_010`): the second read over document nodes is gone, the network builder's `docs/changes/` parse is the one path, and the owner document's Changes section lists them open first then by identifier, each link opening the change in the extension view by `?path=` as a topic opens. Nothing is listed as waiting for an import any more.
- The category carries a filter: a second icon button — Phosphor `funnel`, *Filter changes*, `aria-pressed` for whether the row shows — reveals a row of six toggle icon buttons, one per status in the vocabulary's order (`idea`, `draft`, `ready`, `wip`, `completed`, `rejected`), each `aria-pressed` for whether changes of that status list and named by the status word. The chosen set is stored in the workspace layout beside the category's collapsed state (`filters`, keyed like `sections`), so it follows the workspace across reloads and devices; a workspace that never chose lists `idea`, `draft`, `ready` and `wip` and hides `completed` and `rejected`. Whether the filter row is open is not stored. The funnel reads as active — a mark, not colour alone — whenever the set differs from the default. An open tab of a hidden change stays open: the filter is the list's, not the workspace's. `BO_0222_006`
- The extension view's state read asks the kernel for that extension alone (`BO_0257_009`, `calliopa-bootstrap`'s `BO_0257`): `GET /api/x/ui.shell/extensions/<id>/state` calls `GET /__kernel/extensions/{id}` (`kernelExtensions.one`), which reads that extension's manifest and members only, and passes its `404 extension_unknown` through; it no longer takes the listing of every extension and picks one. The view polls this read while a promotion runs. Measured on the instance 2026-09-16: 1.7–2.7 s before the kernel's read fixes, 0.17–0.19 s through the listing after them.
- The extension view offers, for an extension that declares a trigger, *May start runs on its own* (`BO_0264_018`, `data-extension-autonomous`): a switch the owner alone may press, writing `POST /api/x/ui.shell/extensions/<id>/autonomous`, which the shell's server forwards to the kernel's allowance as the signed-in person; it reads its state from the extension's state read (`autonomous`, `declaresTrigger`, `isOwner`), says to anyone else that the allowance is the owner's, and shows the kernel's words when refused. It changes what runs, not what is served, so nothing is rebuilt.
- The `Extensions` category starts an extension and imports one since `BO_0224`. Its own controls carry a `+` named *New extension*, beside the re-read, the funnel and the import — not the category header's create control, which creates at once and answers what to open, and so can carry no form; the `+` toggles a form in the filter row's idiom: *Name* and *Purpose*, *Create* and *Cancel*, nothing stored across reloads; submitting asks the kernel to establish the extension as truth in one statement — its manifest at version `0.1.0`, `individual`, and a `docs/system/system.md` carrying the purpose — and, when the kernel has answered, the category re-reads and the extension's tab opens on its owner document; the kernel's refusal shows in the form by its code (`extension_id_invalid` with the rule in words — lowercase letters and digits, starting with a letter, in parts joined by a dot or a hyphen — `extension_exists`). For the owner alone a further control — Phosphor `upload-simple`, *Import extension* — opens the browser's file picker for a `.zip`; the file's bytes go to the kernel as they are and the staged import opens as an `extension-import` tab ([Tabs](./tabs.md)). The control renders only when the listing says the session is the owner's, which the reader adds outside the snapshot's cache. `BO_0224_009` `BO_0224_011`
- The shell's icons are Phosphor regular, path data unaltered from `@phosphor-icons/core@2.1.1` (MIT) and inlined in `src/components/shell/icons.tsx` rather than added to the lockfile, rendered `aria-hidden` at 16px inside a control whose accessible name is its label; nothing is drawn by hand. One table serves every status wherever it appears — the filter toggle, the child row, the inspector — so a status looks the same in all three: `lightbulb` for `idea`, `pencil-simple` for `draft`, `flag` for `ready`, `hammer` for `wip`, `check-circle` for `completed`, `x-circle` for `rejected`. `BO_0222_008` A library category's toggle is `caret-right`, turned while the category is expanded, and the Extensions section's re-read is `arrow-clockwise` (`CA_0044_001`, `CA_0044_003`).

### The Library As A Side Bar

* The library reads as VS Code's side bar. Each top-level category is a band that divides the drawer: a header bar reaching both edges, ruled off from the category above by a horizontal line.
* An icon's content scrolls as one. A category's header stays at the content's top while its list scrolls under it, until the next category's header takes its place. User decision, 2026-09-11: one scroll with sticky headers, chosen over VS Code's split panes and over a plain single scroll; since 2026-09-18 it holds within one icon's content rather than across the whole library (`CA_0056_002`).
* Nothing in the library starts left of its parent. A category's rows start on its title's line, and each tree level below starts one step further in.
* Rows within a category sit close, with no space between rows and little padding inside them.
* Horizontal lines separate the categories, the groups inside a category, and an open filter row or create form from the tree below it.
* A row's own action shows while the pointer or the focus is on that row, and always where the device has no hover. User decision, 2026-09-11.
* A component section's own controls sit on its header's line, at the right. They stay with the header while it is sticky and go when the category collapses. User decision, 2026-09-11.

- The side bar is `src/components/shell/shell.css` on the drawer's own markup. `.drawer--left` holds no padding, so bands, hovers, the current row and keyboard focus reach both edges. Custom properties on it name one inset grid:
  - a 0.25rem gutter and a 1rem caret, so a row's text starts 1.5rem in, on its category title's line;
  - a 1rem step per tree level;
  - a 22px row and a 26px header.

  The drawer's text is 0.8125rem, and a title is 0.6875rem, uppercase and bold (`CA_0044_001`, `CA_0044_002`).
- A category's header is sticky at the drawer's top, on a ground mixed from `--panel-raised` into `--panel`. A 1px `--border` rule sits above every category but the first, and below the last. The toggle leads with `caret-right` in the gutter, turned 90° while expanded, and the create control is a `plus` icon button under its unchanged name (`CA_0044_001`).
- A row has no border and no radius, is 22px tall with no gap to the next, and its text is not bold when current. Hover paints `--panel-raised` across the full width. The current row paints `--selection` and keeps its marker dot and `aria-current`. Keyboard focus is an inset 1px `--accent` outline rather than a ring. An empty-state line starts on the rows' inset (`CA_0044_002`).
- In `Extensions`:
  - *Core*, *Yours* and *Other* are small uppercase labels on the inset, each followed by a rule to the right edge.
  - Extension rows sit on the inset, and change rows one step in. Under *Other*, the id it names is one step in and its change two steps in.
  - An extension row paints its hover and its current state across the whole row, its `+` included. Under `(hover: hover)` the *New change* `+` is hidden until the row is hovered or holds focus.
  - The section's controls, with the re-read now `arrow-clockwise` under its unchanged name, ride the header's line ([Contribution Contract](./contribution-contract.md)).
  - The filter row and the create form open under the header, on the inset, ruled off below (`CA_0044_003`).
- The side bar was measured in Chromium (`CA_0044_005`). The page was built from the drawer's markup: the shell's section markup as `shell.tsx` renders it, and the Extensions body as Qwik's render harness renders it (core, individual, pinned, inactive, *Other*, a current change). It was styled with the built stylesheet and the theme, in both themes, at 1280 CSS px on a fine pointer and at 360 CSS px as the phone sheet on a coarse one, where it was measured again after `CA_0044_008`. Every line of every category starts at or right of its title (24px), change rows at 40px under rows at 24px, and *Other*'s change at 56px under its id at 40px. Rows are 22px and headers 26px on both pointers, with no gap between rows. A rule sits above every category but the first. The row `+` is hidden until hovered or focused on the fine pointer, and always shown on the coarse one. With the Extensions list scrolled, its header and its controls both sit at the drawer's top. Collapsed, its controls are gone. An open filter row sits under the header with its rule. `extensions-section.test.ts` presses the section's controls by name unchanged, and the unit project passes.
- The side bar was walked on the served build on a desktop and a phone, and the user set the change completed on 2026-09-11 (`CA_0044_007`). On the desktop at pin 363 the walk-through covered the bands and their rules, a header staying at the top and handing over to the next, the close rows with nothing left of its parent, the row `+` on hover and on keyboard focus, and the Extensions controls working from the header's line. The phone brought two changes: its rows became the desktop's 22px (`CA_0044_008`, pin 366), and the close control's × became 28px in its unchanged box (`CA_0044_009`, pin 369). The user accepted the phone at pin 369.

### Panels As Activity Bars

* Each panel works like VS Code's activity bar and side bar: an icon column on its outer edge and a content area beside it. User decision, 2026-09-18 (`CA_0056`).
* The icon column. The library's column sits at its left edge and the inspector's at its right. The library has one icon per extension that contributes sections, in the reader's order (`CA_0068_001`, below), standing for all of that extension's sections together ([Contribution Contract](./contribution-contract.md)); an extension that is not active contributes nothing and has no icon. The inspector has one icon, Phosphor `info`, for its one section. An icon is a transparent icon button, named by its title with the title as its tooltip; the shown icon is marked by `aria-pressed` and a bar on the column's outer edge, not by colour alone (`CA_0056_001`).
* One content at a time. Clicking an icon shows its content and nothing else. Clicking the icon already shown hides the content and leaves the icon column alone, the small mode. An icon holding one section (Docs, Extensions, Show, the inspector) shows that section with its header — its title and its controls on its line — and no caret. An icon holding several (Publish, Video) names them by the icon's title and shows them as the bands above, each with its sticky header, its caret and its controls, the collapsed state still stored in `Layout.sections` (`CA_0056_002`).
* The panel control. The header's `sidebar-simple` control toggles its whole panel between hidden and shown. Shown brings back what the panel last had: the icon column with the icon that was shown, or the icon column alone. The control carries `aria-pressed` for whether the panel is shown and is named *Library* or *Inspector*; the *Minimum* header menu's two layout controls are the same toggles (`CA_0056_003`).
* What is stored. A panel stores whether it is shown and which icon it shows, or none, in the workspace record, so both follow the workspace across reloads, devices and form factors. The right panel starts hidden in a workspace that never chose; one that stored a state keeps it. The left panel starts shown with its first icon (`CA_0056_004`).

* The library's icons stand in the reader's order. The column draws the extensions its stored order names first, in that order, then every contributing extension it does not name, in contribution order, so a newly activated extension joins at the end and a deactivated one drops out; a workspace that never reordered sees contribution order. User request, 2026-09-24 (`CA_0068_001`).
* An icon is reordered by dragging it within its column: dropped over an icon's upper half it lands before that icon, over its lower half after it, and a line in `--accent` marks the place while the drag lasts. A press without travel stays a press, so a click shows the icon's content as before. Drag is the only way to reorder — no keyboard move and no reset control; dragging back restores an order. User decisions, 2026-09-24 (`CA_0068_002`).
* The order is stored per workspace, beside the panel's state in the workspace record, so it follows the workspace across reloads, devices and form factors and each workspace keeps its own. *The first icon* — the one a workspace that never chose shows, the one an unknown stored id falls back to, and the one a small-mode sheet carries — is the first in the reader's order. User decision, 2026-09-24 (`CA_0068_003`).
- The order is `Layout.libraryOrder` in `src/lib/layout.ts`, extension ids, empty until the reader drags; `parseLayout` in `src/server/workspaces.ts` reads it absent as empty, keeps an id nothing contributes and refuses anything but a list of strings. `orderedIcons` draws the icons in it, `iconDropBefore` names the icon a drop over an icon's half lands before (or `null` for the end), and `movedIconOrder` is the column's order after the drop, unchanged for a drop onto the icon's own place. The shell reads the library's icons through `libraryIcons(layout)` everywhere it read the registry's list — the column, `pressPanelIcon$` and `contentIcon` — so the first icon is the reader's. `PanelIcons` in `src/components/shell/panel.tsx` takes the shell's `startDrag$` and drag store for the library alone: each icon starts a drag of kind `panel-icon` offering `move` on press, is the target `panel-icon:<id>`, and the column is `panel-icon:end`; `targetUnder` in `shell.tsx` turns the half under the pointer into the icon the drop lands before, `dropDrag$` stores `movedIconOrder` and saves the workspace, and each icon draws `data-drop-place` (`before`, `after` on the last for the end, or `none`), which the stylesheet draws as the line. A drag that ends stamps `settledAt`, so the click its lift may send to the icon does not also press it (`CA_0068_005`).
- Tests (`CA_0068_005`): `layout.test.ts` holds the three rules — contribution order with nothing stored, a stored order first with a new icon at the end and a gone or repeated id passed over, the halves, a move before, after and to the end, and a drop onto its own place or of an unknown id leaving the order; `workspaces.test.ts` the order read back, absent as empty and refused when not a list of strings; `panel.test.ts` through `testing/panel-host.tsx` the column drawn in the stored order with the first icon pressed, a press starting a `panel-icon` drag offering `move` with the icons and the column as targets and the inspector's column none, the line on the icon the drop lands before and on the last for the end and none over a tab, and a press without travel still showing content; each shown to fail with its behaviour taken out.
- Measured in Chromium with the built stylesheet and the theme on the column's markup, in both themes, at 1280 CSS px and at 360 with the library's sheet open (`CA_0068_006`): the line is 2px in `--accent` (`#35b8e6` dark, `#0b86b5` light), 36px wide inset 6px each side of the 48px column, straddling the border above the icon the drop lands before (top −1px) or below the last for the end (bottom −1px); the icons and the column draw no dashed outline while dragging; no width scrolls the page. ### Panels Resize At Their Inner Border

* Each panel is resizable at its vertical inner border, the edge its content shares with the workspace: dragging the border changes that panel's content width, and the width holds. The icon column keeps its width. User request, 2026-09-23 (`CA_0066`).
* A width is the browser's, not the workspace's: kept per browser and per side, never sent to the kernel, so a workspace opened on a laptop and on a wide monitor keeps a width on each, and a new browser starts at the default. User decision, 2026-09-23.
* A drag below the minimum stops at the minimum. The handle resizes and does nothing else; the small mode keeps its one way in, the press on the shown icon. User decision, 2026-09-23.
* A double press on the border restores the panel's default width, with a keyboard equivalent on the separator. User decision, 2026-09-23.
- There is no handle in the small mode, hidden, or on a phone, where the panel is a sheet across the one column.
- The rule is `src/lib/layout.ts` beside `pressIcon` and `togglePanel` (`CA_0066_001`): `defaultPanelWidth` is 16rem for the library and 18rem for the inspector at the root's rem, `panelBounds` clamps between `PANEL_MIN_REM` (10rem) and what the viewport leaves after the other panel's whole column, the panel's own icon column and `WORKSPACE_MIN_REM` (20rem) for the workspace — never below the minimum, however little room; a drag (`draggedPanelWidth`, travel toward the workspace widening on either side) and a key (`stepPanelWidth`, `PANEL_STEP_PX` 16px) pass through one `clampPanelWidth`; `resizeKey` reads the arrow toward the workspace as wider, the arrow away as narrower and Enter as the reset. `layout.test.ts` proves the defaults, the bounds at 1280 CSS px with the other panel hidden and shown, the clamp at both ends, the drag on either side, the keys and the step at a bound.
- The handle is `PanelResizeHandle` in `src/components/shell/panel.tsx` (`CA_0066_002`), rendered by `shell.tsx` after each drawer as a grid sibling in the drawer's own area, since the drawer clips what leaves its box. It is a `role="separator"`, `aria-orientation="vertical"`, named *Resize library* or *Resize inspector*, carrying `aria-valuenow` and, once measured, `aria-valuemin` and `aria-valuemax` in px, with a tab stop. A pointer drag captures the pointer on the handle, so leaving the border mid-drag keeps resizing, and marks the handle `data-active`, on which the stylesheet takes every press off the workspace and selects nothing by the travel; the arrow keys step the width; a double press on the handle and Enter on the focused separator restore the default. The bounds are measured at each press and key from the window's width and the other drawer's `offsetWidth`, 0 while hidden, rather than kept. ### The Sheet Gets Out Of The Way

* Landing the reader on a tab closes the phone's sheet. Every path a panel opens a tab by comes through `openTarget$` — a library row, a section's create control, the Extensions section's *New extension* and *Import extension*, the two rows in the inspector, and the header's settings and update controls — so that is the one place it is said. User decision, 2026-09-22 (`CA_0059_001`).
* A press outside an open sheet closes it and does nothing else. The sheet covers what is beneath it, so a press aimed under it is a press whose target the reader cannot see; the next press reaches the block, the tab or the control. ## Inspector
