# Layout

## Desktop Layout

* Header: project identity, horizontally scrollable tabs, process status indicator, layout controls, settings, light/dark toggle, and the signed-in person's menu at the very right.
* The header is always one line, as tall as the laurel and the *Calliopa* wordmark, on both form factors and whatever it holds. On a phone the tab strip below it is the one exception (Mobile Layout).
* Left drawer: hierarchical navigation for projects, stories, acts, scenes, characters, media, and saved views.
* Center workspace: whatever the active tab represents — script editor, storyboard, timeline, scene board, or asset view.
* Right drawer: contextual inspector whose content follows the active item. It is not a second permanent navigation area.
* Bottom command dock: contextual commands, AI requests, uploads, and running-process reporting.
* Both drawers support `expanded`, `compact`, and `hidden` states on desktop.

- The chrome is quiet by default. Layout controls, the theme toggle, and the drawer close controls are transparent icon buttons that gain a surface and border on hover, not bordered form buttons.
- Settings is Phosphor `gear`, and the two layout controls are `sidebar-simple`, the right drawer's mirrored by a CSS transform so the path stays unaltered. Their names are unchanged (`CA_0041_002`).
- The one loud element in the header is the owner's update hint (`BO_0223_013`, 2026-09-09): *Update available: <version>*, bordered in the accent, shown only while the session is the owner's, the build holds the settings extension's `update` kind, the release check is on and GitHub's release list holds a release above the installed one. The check runs in the owner's browser once per session (`src/lib/releases.ts`, remembered in `sessionStorage`) and nowhere in the stack; the kernel's session answer says who the owner is (`src/server/session.ts`, `owner`), and the installed release comes from the settings extension's update read. Off, unreachable, or nothing newer all mean no hint and no error. The hint opens the Update tab.
- The signed-in person is `PersonMenu` (`src/components/shell/header-disclosure.tsx`), the last control on the header's line: a Phosphor `user` button whatever the person's class, named `Signed in as <name>. Account`, whose panel opens under it at its right edge with the name, the role in the header's words (`may establish` for a human, `proposes` otherwise) and *Sign out*, which posts `/__kernel/session/sign-out` and then goes to the kernel's sign-in page. `data-identity` and `data-identity-class` stay on its wrapper (`CA_0041_003`).
- The person's menu and the licence warning share `HeaderDisclosure`, a disclosure rather than an ARIA menu: the button carries `aria-expanded` and `aria-controls`, not `aria-haspopup`, because the panel says who is signed in and what the licence says, and a `role="menu"` may hold nothing but its items. Focus stays on the button, and Tab reaches the panel. A press outside (a `document:` listener, as the agent menu has), Escape anywhere in it, or a second press on the button closes it, and Escape returns focus to the button. The panel sits at z-index 30: above the workspace, the dock and the phone's sheets, below messages and the drag preview. `header.test.ts` presses the button, reads the name, the role and *Sign out*, closes the panel by a second press and by Escape from the button and from inside the panel, and signs out. The press outside is the walk-through's, since the harness dispatches no `document:` listener (`CA_0041_003`).
- The licence warning is `LicenceWarning`: a Phosphor `warning` button in the `error` colour on both form factors, named by the licensor's text, whose panel shows the text. It keeps `data-licence-warning` and its `role="status"` wrapper, and renders for as long as `src/lib/licence.ts` says the warning is live. The render harness proves it, since this instance's licence is in force (`CA_0041_004`).
- The workspace region renders no heading and no view-name tag. A tag naming the tool is chrome the tab strip already supplies, and the headline belongs to the view that owns the content and can edit it, so a title is rendered once by that view. The region names itself directly, keeping its landmark (`CA_0013_001`).
- The command dock has the same three positions on desktop as on mobile, with the dock handle available on both. The desktop dock arrives at composer height and shows the process console only in its console position.
- Dock position is one persisted value per workspace and applies on both form factors, so a position set on one follows the workspace to the other.
- The left drawer arrives `expanded` while the library holds nothing, so the region stays visible and its state cycle stays provable.
- The left drawer holds one top-level category, `Documents`, listing the parentless documents [Block Document Model](../documents/block-document-model.md) answers, each entry naming its document by title. It arrives `expanded`, so the library is visible without being opened.
- Activating an entry opens that document in a tab. A tab already showing the document is revealed rather than opened a second time, because a tab is found by its target rather than by its identity.
- The entry for the active tab's document reads as selected, carrying a marker and `aria-current` rather than colour alone.
- With no document in the graph the category shows an empty-state line under its heading, so the region reads as a place that holds things rather than as something that failed to load.
- The category header carries a small `+` that creates a document and opens it in its own tab, in the chrome idiom above and with its own accessible name. It is the only way to create a document; the header offers none.
- The category is collapsible, and its collapsed state is stored in the workspace record, so it follows the workspace across reloads and devices.
- Documents live in the graph rather than in a workspace, so every workspace lists every document. Scoping the library to a project or a workspace arrives with the change that introduces those.
- The rendered listing is rebuilt rather than reconciled: a read raises a counter the list is keyed by. Entries move and are renamed in the same read, and a keyed diff over that left stale names on reused rows. A tab is keyed by its identity and its title for the same reason, so a renamed target gives its tab a new label.
- A rename re-reads the listing rather than re-sorting it in the browser. Title order is the server's, so the drawer cannot disagree with what the next read would say.
- The `Extensions` category is a tree since `BO_0222`: each extension row carries a small icon button — Phosphor `plus`, named *New change for `<id>`* — that creates a change document of that extension (*Untitled change*, `change` naming the extension, status `idea`) and opens it in the editor; under the row, indented, the extension's change documents, each the icon that stands for its status and its title, opening the document by its identity so one already open is revealed, ordered by title as the Documents category orders; a change naming an extension the graph does not hold lists under a trailing *Other* by the id it names. An inactive extension keeps its control. The category re-reads when a document tab changes, the rule that keeps Documents current. `BO_0222_005`
- The category carries a filter: a second icon button — Phosphor `funnel`, *Filter changes*, `aria-pressed` for whether the row shows — reveals a row of six toggle icon buttons, one per status in the vocabulary's order (`idea`, `draft`, `ready`, `wip`, `completed`, `rejected`), each `aria-pressed` for whether changes of that status list and named by the status word. The chosen set is stored in the workspace layout beside the category's collapsed state (`filters`, keyed like `sections`), so it follows the workspace across reloads and devices; a workspace that never chose lists `idea`, `draft`, `ready` and `wip` and hides `completed` and `rejected`. Whether the filter row is open is not stored. The funnel reads as active — a mark, not colour alone — whenever the set differs from the default. An open tab of a hidden change stays open: the filter is the list's, not the workspace's. `BO_0222_006`
- The `Extensions` category starts an extension and imports one since `BO_0224`. Its own controls carry a `+` named *New extension*, beside the re-read, the funnel and the import — not the category header's create control, which creates at once and answers what to open, and so can carry no form; the `+` toggles a form in the filter row's idiom: *Name* and *Purpose*, *Create* and *Cancel*, nothing stored across reloads; submitting asks the kernel to establish the extension as truth in one statement — its manifest at version `0.1.0`, `individual`, and a `docs/system/system.md` carrying the purpose — and, when the kernel has answered, the category re-reads and the extension's tab opens on its owner document; the kernel's refusal shows in the form by its code (`extension_id_invalid` with the rule in words — lowercase letters and digits, starting with a letter, in parts joined by a dot or a hyphen — `extension_exists`). For the owner alone a further control — Phosphor `upload-simple`, *Import extension* — opens the browser's file picker for a `.zip`; the file's bytes go to the kernel as they are and the staged import opens as an `extension-import` tab ([Tabs](./tabs.md)). The control renders only when the listing says the session is the owner's, which the reader adds outside the snapshot's cache. `BO_0224_009` `BO_0224_011`
- The shell's icons are Phosphor regular, path data unaltered from `@phosphor-icons/core@2.1.1` (MIT) and inlined in `src/components/shell/icons.tsx` rather than added to the lockfile, rendered `aria-hidden` at 16px inside a control whose accessible name is its label; nothing is drawn by hand. One table serves every status wherever it appears — the filter toggle, the child row, the inspector — so a status looks the same in all three: `lightbulb` for `idea`, `pencil-simple` for `draft`, `flag` for `ready`, `hammer` for `wip`, `check-circle` for `completed`, `x-circle` for `rejected`. `BO_0222_008`

## Mobile Layout

* Only one primary surface dominates at a time.
* Tabs form a swipeable, horizontally scrolling strip below the compact header.
* Left and right drawers become edge sheets.
* The command dock has three vertical positions: collapsed handle, composer, expanded process console. Swiping the dock up or down changes its position. A tap on the handle closes an open dock and opens a collapsed one to the composer.
* Long-press initiates drag; ordinary touch movement scrolls.
* System back gestures remain available: drawer gestures begin from visible handles or sufficiently inset regions.
* The header takes no more height than its line and the tab strip below it. It keeps reserving space on both sides for the library and inspector sheet handles, which sit centered vertically on the line at the left and right edges.

- The left drawer's edge handle opens the library as a sheet, so the drawer keeps its landmark, its accessible name, and its gesture coverage on a phone.
- The mobile sheet carries the `Documents` category as well, so the drawer holds the same library on a phone as on a desktop while keeping its close control and its gesture coverage.
- On a phone the owner's update hint is Phosphor `arrow-circle-up` inside the hint's accent border, with its words hidden; its name is unchanged, and the desktop shows the words with the icon hidden (`CA_0041_005`).
- The header's line is the wordmark's height. `--header-line` (the laurel's 1.55em at the header's 1.2rem) and `--header-pad` on `.shell` name it, and every button on the line centres its icon or count, so none is taller; the process count has a set `line-height`, so no monospace face can raise it. Measured in Chromium with the built stylesheet on the header's markup as the render harness renders it, with every control shown: at 1280 and 360 CSS px the wordmark (29.8px) is the tallest item and every item's centre is on one line, and the desktop header is 46.8px (`CA_0041_007`).
- On a phone `.shell-header` still wraps, but only the tab strip goes below. The wordmark takes `flex: 1 1 0`, so it absorbs what the controls leave rather than pushing one onto a second line, and it clips rather than overlaps if a narrower screen leaves it too little. The gap and the buttons' inline padding are 0.2rem. The sheet handles' top is computed from `--header-pad`, `--header-line` and their own 2.75rem, so their middle is the line's. Measured at 360 CSS px with the owner's full set shown: the controls end at the 36px reserve, the wordmark keeps its full width with about 10px to spare, the tab strip is the only row below (header 73.1px), and both handles' centres sit at the wordmark's, 22.9px (`CA_0041_008`).
- The one-line header was walked on the served build at pin 307 on a desktop and a phone, and the user set the change completed on 2026-09-11. The walk-through covered the checks the harness and the fixture measurement could not reach: the line at the wordmark's height with the tabs alone below it on the phone, the handles centred on the line and opening their sheets, the theme icons cycling, the person menu opening and closing all three ways (a press outside included) with *Sign out* landing on the sign-in page, the phone's save-state and update icons, and the gear and sidebar icons. The licence warning stays proven in the harness alone, since this instance's licence is in force (`CA_0041_009`).

## Inspector

* The right drawer renders the active view's contributed facts and actions when no process is selected. The shell renders them in its own idiom; the view says what they are.

- The contribution is typed facts and named actions, defined in [Workspace View Types](./view-types.md). The shell knows how to render a save state, a time, a count, a line of text, a button, and a toggle. It does not know what a document is or what deleting one means, so the view supplies the handlers.
- A selected process takes the inspector of the tab it was selected on, and no other. Each tab keeps its own selection, as it keeps its own drawer context ([Tabs](./tabs.md#tabs)), and the empty workspace keeps its own while no tab is open. Switching tabs, including opening a document, shows that tab's selected process or else its view's contribution. Coming back to a tab shows its process again. A closed tab takes its selection with it, so a tab opened again on the same target starts with none (`CA_0040_001`).
- The selection is held in the page and never in the workspace record, and not in the tab's persisted `drawerContext` either. Process detail is transient, so a reload starts with nothing selected (`CA_0040_001`).
- The process detail ends with a labelled *Close process* button that lets the tab's selection go and brings back its view's contribution. It is a word rather than a ×, because the phone's inspector sheet already carries a × that closes the sheet. On a phone the sheet stays open and shows the contribution (`CA_0040_002`).
- A view that contributes nothing shows nothing, which is what a placeholder view does today.
- The inspector is reachable in each of its desktop drawer states and as the mobile edge sheet, so whatever a view contributes is reachable on both form factors. A `hidden` drawer hides it, which is what hiding a drawer means.

## Implementation

- The per-tab selection is `src/lib/process-selection.ts` (`selectedProcess`, `pressProcess`, `releaseProcess`, `keepOpenTabs`), held on the shell's process registry beside its items. The shell prunes it when the tabs change, and reads what the active tab's selected process proposed. The inspector's body and the console's process list are `InspectorPanel` and `ProcessList` in `src/components/shell/inspector.tsx`. `process-selection.test.ts` presses them in the render harness through a real `.tsx` host (`testing/inspector-host.tsx`): a tab switch, *Close process*, and a second press on a row, each shown to fail when its behaviour is taken out (`CA_0040_001`–`CA_0040_003`). On the served build, on a desktop and on a phone, the reader released a process both ways, kept a selection across a tab switch and back, and set a change document's status in the inspector (`CA_0040_004`).
- The desktop shell grid presents identity and tabs in the header, the empty library drawer, placeholder working context, contextual inspector, and command dock. Each drawer independently cycles expanded, compact, and hidden; Given/When/Then browser coverage verifies the regions, state cycle, and accessibility (`CA_0002_014`).
- On phones, the compact header places the horizontally swipeable tab strip beneath identity controls, drawers open as edge sheets from visible inset handles, and the workspace remains dominant. The dock cycles or swipes among collapsed, composer, and process-console positions; unit and mobile browser coverage prove the bounded gestures (`CA_0002_015`). Until `BO_0230` the tap cycled forward through the three, so from the composer it opened the console; it now closes an open dock and opens a collapsed one, and the console is a swipe up or the header's control (`BO_0230_002`).
- The left drawer renders nothing: no node kinds, no list, and no heading. It keeps its region, its `Library` name, its desktop state cycle, its mobile edge handle, and the sheet close control that is its only child. Desktop coverage asserts no visible content in the drawer; mobile asserts the close control is the only thing in the sheet (`CA_0010_001`).
- Layout controls, the theme toggle, the sheet close controls, and the tab close and open controls are transparent icon buttons that take the raised surface and a border on hover. Workspace and dock buttons keep their resting border (`CA_0010_004`).
- The inspector renders a view's contributed facts as a description list and its actions as buttons, in the shell's own idiom: a save state in words, a time as a `time` element carrying the machine-readable stamp, a count, a toggle as an `aria-pressed` button, and a destructive action marked as one. A contribution with neither facts nor actions falls back to the view's declared line. It is its own component for the reason the header's save state is: re-rendering the shell would rebuild the mounted view, and for an editing surface that means rebuilding the element the caret is in (`CA_0015_006`).
- A tab switch clears the contribution along with the save state. What the previous view contributed was for the previous tab, so an unreported tab falls back to its declared line rather than showing another document's facts under this tab's name (`CA_0015_006`).
- The `Mark unsaved` and `Move first` placeholder buttons are gone, along with the `.context-actions` group that held them. A real view reports its save state through the bridge and tabs reorder by drag, so both were demos of behaviour that has since arrived for real. Their scenarios went with them: per-tab unsaved isolation is deliberately left unproven, because a two-document scenario buys a slow browser test for the marker's projection and giving the placeholder views a save state would keep a reported state standing for nothing being saved (`CA_0015_006`).
- The Extensions tree, the row's `+` and the *Other* group are `src/components/library/extensions-section.tsx` over `listExtensions()`, whose answer carries each extension's change documents read as the documents listing reads (`listChangeDocuments`) and never from the pinned snapshot; the section declares `kind: "document"`, and `refreshSection$` now re-reads a component section too, handing the answer down as its data, which the component follows (`BO_0222_005`).
- The filter set is `Layout.filters`, parsed beside `sections` with the same renamed-key rule and preserved for a section nothing contributes; `SectionProps` gained `filter` and `setFilter$`, so a component section reads and stores its own set in its own vocabulary and the shell judges none of the values; the pure rules — the default, a stored set read back in the vocabulary's order with unknown values dropped, a toggle, the grouping under extensions and *Other* — are `src/lib/changes.ts` with their unit test (`BO_0222_006`).
- The create form, the import control and the export are `src/components/library/extensions-section.tsx` (the form's rule in `src/lib/extension-id.ts`; the control is the section's own, and `src/components/library/extensions-section.test.ts` presses it, *Cancel* and the funnel in Qwik's render harness), `src/server/kernel/extensions.ts` (`create`, `exportArchive`, `importArchive`, `importRecord`, `promote`, `decide`) behind `POST /api/x/ui.shell/extensions`, `GET …/extensions/[id]/export`, `POST …/extensions/import`, `GET …/extensions/import/[group]`, `POST …/extensions/import/[group]/accept|reject` and `POST …/extensions/promote` in `contributions.server.ts`; the summary's lines and the next step are `src/lib/extension-import.ts` with their unit test; `upload-simple` and `download-simple` join the icon table (`BO_0224_009`, `BO_0224_010`, `BO_0224_011`, `BO_0224_012`).
- The inspector and the dock render a view's `choice` action as a labelled native select with the current value's icon beside it (`ChoiceControl` in `inspector.tsx`), the third kind of the one action vocabulary (`BO_0222_007`).
